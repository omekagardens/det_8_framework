## 2. Kernel-native silent congruences: admissibility, not a selected law

For positive integer dimensions, write \(e_n=(1,\ldots,1)^T\),
\(\Gamma_n=e_ne_n^\dagger\) and

\[
K_n=\{d\in\mathrm{Herm}_n:d\succeq0\},\qquad
m_n(d)=e_n^\dagger d e_n=\operatorname{Tr}(\Gamma_n d).
\tag{1}
\]

The symbol \(\Gamma_n\) is not the completion's encoding map \(J_n\).
For a square matrix \(M\), the congruence \(S_M(d)=MdM^\dagger\) preserves
positivity. It preserves mass on all of \(K_n\) exactly when

\[
M^\dagger\Gamma_nM=\Gamma_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
M^\dagger e_n=e^{i\theta}e_n
\tag{2}
\]

for some real \(\theta\). The first equivalence follows by evaluating mass
against every positive matrix; the second equates the two nonzero rank-one
outer products. If \(M\) is invertible, its inverse supplies a positive,
mass-preserving inverse congruence. These properties compose. Tensor products
also preserve (2) under the already-declared product-carrier construction.
This is not a proof of physically independent subsystem availability.

This gives nontrivial algebraically admissible silent transformations without
inserting a physical Hilbert space, Hamiltonian or metric. But it does **not**
force ordinary unitary dynamics. In two alternatives, set

\[
w=(1,-1)^T,\qquad P_+=\tfrac12e_2e_2^T,\quad
P_-=\tfrac12ww^T,\qquad M_\lambda=P_++\lambda P_-,\quad\lambda>0.
\tag{3}
\]

Then \(M_\lambda e_2=e_2\), \(M_\lambda^{-1}=M_{1/\lambda}\), but

\[
M_\lambda^\dagger M_\lambda=P_++\lambda^2P_-\ne I
\quad\text{if }\lambda\ne1.
\]

Thus positivity, mass preservation and reversibility allow a noncompact
scaling family, not just the unitary group. No value of \(\lambda\), sequence
of these maps, physical rate or duration has been selected. A permutation
can be merely passive relabeling if the entire interface is relabeled too;
an active operation requires independently specified, fixed record tests.

**Answer so far:** DET's admitted matrix conditions constrain this class of
silent candidates, but neither establish their physical availability nor
identify which one occurs. They do not derive a quantum evolution law.

## 3. Full-cone instrument no-information theorem

Suppose finitely many fixed real-linear maps

\[
T_r:\mathrm{Herm}_n\longrightarrow\mathrm{Herm}_{n_r}
\]

are positive on the **entire** \(K_n\), and satisfy

\[
\sum_r m_{n_r}(T_rd)=1
\quad\text{for every }d\succeq0\text{ with }m_n(d)=1.
\tag{4}
\]

An index \(r\) can include the silent alternative or the entire fresh-append
branch, including precursor ideal and payload. No scalar-only substitution
for that branch is being made.

**Theorem.** There are constants \(p_r\ge0\), with \(\sum_rp_r=1\), such that

\[
m_{n_r}(T_rd)=p_r m_n(d)\quad\text{for every }d\succeq0.
\tag{5}
\]

Consequently all normalized residual inputs give the same branch probabilities.
Complete positivity is not needed; the obstruction already holds for positivity.

**Proof.** Let \(E_r=T_r^*(\Gamma_{n_r})\), using the real trace pairing on
Hermitian matrices. Positivity of \(T_r\) implies \(E_r\succeq0\).
Equation (4) extends to positive inputs of nonzero mass by scaling. For
\(z\succeq0\) of zero mass, choose normalized \(d_0\). Both \(d_0\) and
\(d_0+z\) are normalized. Subtracting their completeness identities proves
the identity also on \(z\). Since positive matrices span the Hermitian space,

\[
\sum_rE_r=\Gamma_n,\qquad 0\preceq E_r\preceq\Gamma_n.
\]

For any \(v\perp e_n\), \(0\le v^\dagger E_rv\le
v^\dagger\Gamma_nv=0\). A positive matrix with zero quadratic form on \(v\)
annihilates \(v\); hence \(E_r\) is supported on the line spanned by \(e_n\).
Thus \(E_r=p_r\Gamma_n\), proving (5) and the stated normalization. \(\square\)

This is the familiar rank-one domination fact applied to this candidate's
normalization, not a theorem that informative DET records are impossible.
Dropping singular matrices alone is not a remedy: arbitrary positive
zero-mass additions to a positive-definite normalized input remain positive
definite and normalized. Positivity/identities extend to the boundary by
finite-dimensional continuity.

**Sequential consequence.** At each fixed history/context suppose subsequent
branches again act on full cones with these properties. Every finite branch-word
probability is then a product of the corresponding state-independent constants.
Controls chosen from the recorded history do not change this conclusion for
two inputs at the same initial history/context. Summing silent words cannot
restore residual dependence. This argument does not apply if a later test
is only defined on a restricted domain: that leaves the candidate class.

For one stationary silent map \(Q\) and commit maps \(B_r\), write

\[
mQ=qm,\qquad m_rB_r=b_rm,\qquad q+\sum_rb_r=1.
\]

Then \(P(n,r)=b_rq^n\). For \(q<1\), eventual commitment has probability one
and \(P(r\mid\mathrm{commit})=b_r/(1-q)\); for \(q=1\), no commit occurs.
Neither alternative depends on the initial normalized kernel. Here \(n\) is
supplied composition depth, not physical time. In particular this construction
cannot generate residual-dependent first-commit or precursor-selection laws.

## 4. Why probability convergence is insufficient for residual elimination

The preceding [first-commit lemma](../../track_b/QR_MAP_RECORD_SUFFICIENCY.md#5-first-commit-elimination-for-a-supplied-finite-instrument)
proved convergence of the summed CP maps on **trace-normalized quantum
systems**. Its Choi trace bound must not be copied to unrestricted \(m\).

Use (3)'s projections and the exact matrices

\[
A=\tfrac12P_++2P_-=\begin{pmatrix}5/4&-3/4\\-3/4&5/4\end{pmatrix},
\quad Q(d)=AdA^T,\quad B(d)=\tfrac34d,
\]
\[
d_0=\tfrac12P_++P_-=\begin{pmatrix}3/4&-1/4\\-1/4&3/4\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{6}
\]

These maps are positive (even completely positive), \(m(d_0)=1\),
\(mQ=\tfrac14m\), \(mB=\tfrac34m\), and their sum conserves mass. Yet

\[
H_N(d_0):=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}BQ^n(d_0)
=\tfrac12(1-4^{-N})P_++\tfrac14(4^N-1)P_-.
\tag{7}
\]

Orthogonality of \(P_+,P_-\) gives (7) by two geometric sums. Its total-entry
mass tends to one, while its coefficient of \(P_-\) diverges. Thus no finite
matrix limit of \(H_N(d_0)\), and hence no finite map limit, exists. The
positive zero-mass direction is invisible to \(m\), not bounded by it.

Also, a zero-probability map need not be the zero map: for nonzero positive
\(z\) with \(m(z)=0\), \(T(d)=m(d)z\) is a nonzero positive map with zero
output mass. Such a branch is never normalized or committed. This is why
the compatible completion's “zero trace implies zero positive output” rule
is not a theorem about unrestricted kernels.

**Conclusion:** convergence of first-record probabilities does not by itself
authorize eliminating silent activity while retaining the full residual
payload. The earlier trace-based theorem remains valid in its stated domain.

## 5. Existing informative records do not contradict this theorem

**Exactly decoherent partition domains.** On
\(d=\operatorname{diag}(p,1-p)\), the singleton partition produces weights
\(p,1-p\), so it is informative. The stated commit rule is licensed on its
recordable domain, not on every normalized positive kernel. For example

\[
d=\begin{pmatrix}4&-2\\-2&1\end{pmatrix}=(2,-1)^T(2,-1)
\]

is positive with mass one, yet the singleton weights are \(4,1\). Their sum
is five, and the partition is not decoherent. These raw quadratic weights
are not licensed commit probabilities. No renormalization is silently added.
The existing four-alternative \(d(c)\), \(c=\pm1/8\), similarly retains its
exact \(3/4\) versus \(1/4\) readout on its common recordable partition.

Restricting a cone changes the allowed dual effects. A recordable restricted
cone can still contain nonzero zero-mass elements and support informative
effects; faithful normalization is **not** asserted to be the only escape.
Physical selection and transitions between such domains remain to be specified.

**Compatible quantum image cones.** In the conditional
[finite-QM representation](../t8-q-operational-completion-2026-09-12/RECONSTRUCTION.md#3-full-quantum-calculus-in-faithful-pair-kernel-coordinates),

\[
\mathcal C_n=J_n(\mathrm{PSD}_n),\qquad
m(J_n\rho)=\operatorname{Tr}\rho.
\]

On that image, the transported instruments are positive, informative and
normalized. They need not have positive mass-contracting extensions to the
full ambient PSD cone; the theorem proves that an informative complete family
cannot have such an extension. Finite-dimensional injectivity and continuity
of \(J_n\) also transport the prior trace-based first-commit convergence
within these image cones. This uses the chosen operational representation,
not a derived selection of it from B_D.

**Other explicit changes.** Nonlinear or state-dependent operation rules
leave the fixed-linear candidate; mixture consistency and composition would
need separate justification. Replacing total-entry normalization by trace
changes the kernel's operational interpretation rather than repairing this
candidate under its original premises. No such change is adopted here.

it. Neither calling a kernel “live” nor demanding positivity selects the
quantum domain.
