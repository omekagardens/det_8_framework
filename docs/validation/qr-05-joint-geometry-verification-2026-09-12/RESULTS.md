## The structural ambiguity survives the joint test

At any fixed κ,ε, combine `(a,b)=(1,0)` or `(0,1)` with
`(L,r)=(1,1)` or `(3/2,2/3)`. The four worlds have identical coordinate
intensity coefficients, normalized point density and O,P,T, but four different
complete geometric targets. These are simultaneous shape and scale collisions,
not a conclusion extrapolated from unrelated axis slices.

Under the design's stipulated Poisson premise, equal intensity means equal
complete point-process law; this law-level implication is analytical, not a
simulated count-tail result. It need not hold for an arbitrary correlated
process. Fixed-quota point records likewise cannot reveal the missing total
intensity. D separates the shape exchange and R separates the scale/rate
compensation. Neither ideal reference is an acquired measurement here.

The named transformation `L → (3/2)L`, `r → (2/3)r` was checked on every fixed
world in both engines. It preserves intensity and O,P,T,D, multiplies absolute
volume by 3/2, and changes R. Conditional on κ,a,b, the record-channel Fisher
matrix is `T[[1,1],[1,1]]`. Its null direction `(1,−1)` survives the checked
independent repetition factors 1 and 3; the prior analytical argument covers
any such repetition. This is an information deficit, not spacetime curvature.

A common quantum encoding or postprocessing cannot split identical input laws
under the prior fixed-encoding assumptions. No quantum executor ran in this
gate. A new world-dependent probe would be an additional channel requiring
its own contract. Population-law identification is also distinct from
error-free finite-record inference, calibration or a validated RET workflow.
