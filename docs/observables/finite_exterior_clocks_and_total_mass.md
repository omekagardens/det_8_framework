# Can a finite exterior clock array determine total active mass?

Conditional theorem accepted after independent scientific review on 9 November 2026.

**No fixed finite array of exterior Newtonian potential readings identifies total active mass over all centered, inversion-symmetric positive sources in the ball.** This holds even with exact absolute potentials, a fixed potential reference, known coupling and perfect calibration. At the original three clock locations, two registered three-sector worlds can agree on every recorded outcome while only one has active totals proportional to its inertias. The common clock readings in the example also pass the earlier spherical-model consistency check.

## Source and clock conventions

Let $\mu$ be a nonnegative Borel measure of finite, strictly positive total mass, compactly supported inside the radius-$a$ ball about the known origin in three dimensions. Assume $\mu(E)=\mu(-E)$. The first moment then vanishes. A unit orbit $P_y=(\delta_y+\delta_{-y})/2$ splits its mass equally between the two reflected points, with $P_0=\delta_0$ counted once. Positive mixtures of these orbits are admitted. Sector inertias are independently supplied positive coefficients. Registration fixes the identity of the complete package in each sector across measurement modes. Its active density is unknown.

Subtract the common known background and reference offsets. At retained order the source potential is $\Phi_\mu(x)=-G\int |x-y|^{-1}\,d\mu(y)$ with known $G>0$. The two original signed clock readings at $2a,3a,4a$ on one ray are $g[\Phi(2a)-\Phi(3a)]/c^2$ and $g[\Phi(4a)-\Phi(3a)]/c^2$ with known common positive $g,c$. We take the allowed zero-background case for the explicit worlds. The same known background can be restored cell by cell.

## A collision at the original two clock channels

Let $e_x$ be the clock-ray direction and $e_y$ a perpendicular unit vector. Use four fixed unit-mass orbits
$$
 P_0,\qquad P_L=P_{(a/2)e_x},\qquad P_H=P_{(a/\sqrt2)e_x},\qquad
 P_\perp=P_{(a/2)e_y}.
$$
For dimensionless clock radius $r=2,3,4$, the dimensionless Newton kernel $a/|ra e_x-y|$, averaged on an axial pair at $\pm ta e_x$, has value $r/(r^2-t^2)$. On $P_\perp$ it has value $t_r=(r^2+1/4)^{-1/2}$.

Choose any mass scale $m>0$. Define the two physical sources
$$
 \mu=m(2385P_L+\lambda P_\perp),\qquad
 \nu=m(1344P_0+1054P_H+\lambda P_\perp).                                  \tag{1}
$$
The common perpendicular weight makes their readings agree with a spherical fit as well as each other. Its explicit value is
$$
 B=3t_3-t_2-2t_4,\qquad \lambda=\frac{212}{7B}.
$$
To check $B>0$, define $b(v)=3(9+v)^{-1/2}-(4+v)^{-1/2}-2(16+v)^{-1/2}$. Then $b(0)=0$ and
$$
 2b'(v)=(4+v)^{-3/2}+2(16+v)^{-3/2}-3(9+v)^{-3/2}>0,\quad 0\le v\le1/4.
$$
Indeed $(17/4)^{-3/2}>1/9\ge 3(9+v)^{-3/2}$. Thus $B=b(1/4)>0$ and every mass in (1) is positive. The respective totals are **$m(2385+\lambda)$ and $m(2398+\lambda)$**, differing by $13m$. Both sources are centered and every atom is strictly inside the ball.

To check the observations, write $V_\rho(r)=-a\Phi_\rho(ra e_x)/(Gm)$ for either source. The axial formula gives all three sample values directly.

| Source | $V(2)-\lambda t_2$ | $V(3)-\lambda t_3$ | $V(4)-\lambda t_4$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\mu$ | $8904/7$ | $5724/7$ | $4240/7$ |
| $\nu$ | $8920/7$ | $5740/7$ | $4256/7$ |

For example the first row is $2385\,r/(r^2-1/4)$ and the second is $1344/r+1054\,r/(r^2-1/2)$. The difference between rows is $16/7$ at each radius. Thus their absolute potentials differ by the same constant at the three sensors under a fixed reference. Both **raw clock differences** coincide. In units $gGm/(ac^2)$ their common signed pair is
$$
 \left(-3180/7-\lambda(t_2-t_3),\quad 212+\lambda(t_3-t_4)\right).         \tag{2}
$$
The inner reading plus twice the outer reading equals $-212/7+\lambda B=0$. Calling the positive outer component $q=212+\lambda(t_3-t_4)$, (2) is exactly $(-2q,q)$. A central monopole of mass $12mq$ would give this same pair. Hence even verifying the signed spherical relation at these locations cannot supply the missing shape certificate.

## The registered three-sector experiment

Write $(n_0,n_1,n_2)=(1,2,4)$. In both worlds the registered sector inertias are $M_i=M_*n_i$ with $M_*>0$. For a common tick $T>0$, choose the same stiffness $K=M_*/T^2$ and the same rest release with $0<D<L$. Then $u_i=KT^2/M_i=1/n_i$. The squared phases are $3/2,5/4,3/4$ in settings $01,02,12$. They are strictly between $0$ and $\pi^2$, and both worlds give $y_{ij}=\cos\sqrt{u_i+u_j}$.

| World | Registered active profiles $(0,1,2)$ | True total active masses divided by $m$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P | $(\mu,2\mu,4\mu)$ | $(2385+\lambda,\ 2(2385+\lambda),\ 4(2385+\lambda))$ |
| N | $(\mu,2\nu,4\mu)$ | $(2385+\lambda,\ 2(2398+\lambda),\ 4(2385+\lambda))$ |

In P all three active-to-inertial ratios equal $m(2385+\lambda)/M_*$. In N the middle ratio is larger by $13m/M_*$ than the other two. There is no hidden exchange of sector labels. In each world the static and mechanical preparations refer to equivalent copies of the same registered sector package. The physical spatial profile of sector 1 differs between worlds, while the same supplied inertia, gain, background, geometry and native law apply.

Both worlds record $z_i=n_i[gGm/(ac^2)](-2q,q)$ at each logged static setting. With the earlier spherical inversion the inferred active profile would be proportional to $n_i$ in both. To check full-law equality, let $p_i(h)$ be the accepted eight-history law for the preparation $\delta_{00},\delta_{10},\delta_{11}$ respectively. For $h=(j,b_1,b_2)$ it is $(q_j/4)(1+\eta_j\mathbf1_{\{i=1\}}/2)$, with $(q_E,\eta_E)=(2/3,1/8448)$ and $(q_F,\eta_F)=(1/3,-1/4224)$. For every allowed setting and Borel readout event the two worlds have the same probabilities
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 P(I,ij,h_1,h_2,Y\in S,F=0)&=\tfrac16 p_i(h_1)p_j(h_2)\mathbf1_S(y_{ij}),\\
 P(A,i,h,Z\in E,F=0)&=\tfrac16 p_i(h)\mathbf1_E(z_i).
 \end{aligned}
$$
There are 192 labelled mechanical cells and 24 static cells, with total mass one. All other support, including $F=1$, has zero mass in the ideal design. The collision preserves both native histories of each mechanical pair, the full history in each static cell and both signed clock readings.

The same source collision also shows how a spherical fit could falsely reject proportionality. Write $D_P=2385+\lambda$, $D_N=2398+\lambda$. For this implication give *both* worlds the common inertia triple $M_*(1,2D_N/D_P,4)$ instead. With the same $KT^2=M_*$, the middle inverse inertia is now $D_P/(2D_N)<1/2$, so every pair still satisfies the first-lobe condition. The worlds again have identical mechanical records to each other. World N is now truly proportional, while the fitted monopole profile $1:2:4$ would exclude proportionality to this inertia triple. Both directions of error use the same four source orbits and the same two signed clock channels.

All sampled points on the original segment lie at distance at least $a$ from the support. For any sector, $|\Phi|/c^2\le G\mu_{\rm sector}(\mathbb R^3)/(ac^2)$. Choose one sufficiently small positive $m$ across both worlds to meet any prescribed positive potential-amplitude tolerance there. Equality of paired records and the inequality of the true mass ratios both survive. The calculation concerns the exterior sampled region of a supplied static potential.

## The exact criterion for any fixed finite array

Work in potential units after applying the known gains, offsets and backgrounds. For an exterior sensor at $x$, its absolute inverse-distance response on a unit symmetric orbit is the function of **source position** $y$
$$
       K_x(y)=\tfrac12\left(|x-y|^{-1}+|x+y|^{-1}\right).
$$
With a fixed absolute potential reference, each measured absolute potential supplies $K_x$, up to its known factor $-G$. A measured difference between $x$ and $x'$ supplies $K_x-K_{x'}$ with the specified ordering. For a mixed array use precisely the actual rows. An unmeasured reference potential supplies no extra absolute row. Repeated or linearly dependent rows are allowed.

Let $f_1,\ldots,f_N$ be these observed kernels and $v(y)=(f_1(y),\ldots,f_N(y))$. By symmetry the observations of $\rho$ are $L\rho=\int v(y)\,d\rho(y)$, and $LP_y=v(y)$. A reconstruction of total mass valid for **all** allowed sources, even a nonlinear reconstruction, exists if and only if
$$
             1\in\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{f_1,\ldots,f_N\}
             \quad\text{as functions on the source ball}.                 \tag{3}
$$
If $1=\sum_j c_j f_j$, integration gives $\rho(\mathbb R^3)=\sum_j c_j (L\rho)_j$.

For the converse, here is a finite-support construction whenever (3) fails. Choose orbit positions $y_1,\ldots,y_r$ so that $v(y_k)$ form a basis of the span of all response vectors, with $r\le N$. Let $\ell$ be the linear functional on that span assigning one to each basis vector. If $\ell(v(y))=1$ for every interior $y$, extending $\ell$ to $\mathbb R^N$ would give (3). So choose an interior $y_*$ for which $\ell(v(y_*))\ne1$. Write $v(y_*)=\sum_{k=1}^r c_k v(y_k)$. The signed combination
$$
                \xi=P_{y_*}-\sum_{k=1}^r c_k P_{y_k}
$$
has zero measurement vector and total mass $1-\sum_k c_k=1-\ell(v(y_*))\ne0$. This argument uses the source ball's interior, which already lies within the allowed support. Continuous kernels extend any identity on the interior to its closure.

Collect positive and negative orbit weights into nonnegative symmetric measures $\xi_+$ and $\xi_-$ with $\xi=\xi_+-\xi_-$. Taking $\rho_1=\xi_++P_0$ and $\rho_2=\xi_-+P_0$ guarantees two strictly positive totals even when one part is empty. Their readings agree, their totals differ, and both centers are the prescribed origin. If $r=0$ this construction uses an empty basis and $\xi=P_{y_*}$. An overall mass unit and common positive rescaling can be applied to both. On any fixed finite exterior array their potentials are finite, so a sufficiently small common rescaling meets a local weak-field tolerance. This constructs a collision for the **actual rows**, including every absolute reading for an absolute array. No function of the common data can return both unequal totals.

**Evaluation for the Newton kernel.** List all sensor and reference positions occurring in the rows as $x_1,\ldots,x_m$. Every $f_j$ is real analytic in $y$ on the connected open set
$$
             \Omega=\mathbb R^3\setminus\{\pm x_1,\ldots,\pm x_m\}.
$$
Indeed each inverse distance is real analytic away from its point singularity. Removing finitely many points leaves path-connected three-dimensional space. Sensor and reflected sensor positions are exterior, so $\Omega$ contains the open source ball. If a finite linear combination equalled one there, the identity theorem for real analytic functions on a connected open set would make it equal one throughout $\Omega$. As $|y|\to\infty$ every $K_{x_j}(y)$ tends to zero, and every fixed finite linear combination of absolute kernels and their differences does likewise. This contradicts the constant one. Analytic continuation here concerns the known kernel functions and imposes no assumption about physical sources or the weak-field approximation at infinity.

Thus (3) fails for every such finite array. The explicit example (1) matches two differences and has different absolute samples. The basis construction supplies the absolute-array counterexamples for their own specified sensor positions. Each array has some ambiguous data. The theorem does not compute possible totals for each individual record. A colliding pair for any array can also be embedded by the sector scaling and single-sector substitution above.

## One observation that recovers the monopole

Supply the continuous solid-angle average of the calibrated **outer-minus-inner** clock difference on two complete concentric spheres with distinct known radii $R_2>R_1>a$. Use the same scale, reference convention and known background subtraction over both spheres. Define
$$
 \overline Z_{21}=\frac{g}{4\pi c^2}\int_{S^2}
       [\Phi_\mu(R_2\hat n)-\Phi_\mu(R_1\hat n)]\,d\Omega(\hat n).
$$
For any source point at radius $s<R$, orient the polar axis along it. Its unit-mass kernel has solid-angle mean
$$
 \frac12\int_{-1}^1 (R^2+s^2-2Rst)^{-1/2}\,dt
    =\frac{(R+s)-(R-s)}{2Rs}=\frac1R
$$
for $s>0$, with the same value directly at $s=0$. The exterior kernel is bounded on the integration domain, so integrating over the finite source measure gives the averaged potential $-G\mu(\mathbb R^3)/R$. Consequently
$$
           \mu(\mathbb R^3)=
           \frac{c^2\,\overline Z_{21}}{gG(1/R_1-1/R_2)} .
$$
This is an ideal surface observation with specified solid-angle weights. A finite point quadrature still obeys the obstruction (3) on the full source class. A spherical-shape restriction supplies different information by fixing the exterior response per unit mass. Applying either route physically would require separate evidence for its metrology, source boundary, static frame and controlled remainders. The finite theorem uses the stipulated Newtonian attachment and records.
