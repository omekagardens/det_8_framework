# What finite Newtonian acceleration readings determine about central density

Six equal, positive axial readings permit every central density from zero up to the reported finite-trace density. One additional signed acceleration component determines the density and the mass at each corner separately within the specified two-source family.

## Field, geometry and observation

At one inertial instant the preparation consists of a ball of uniform density ρ ≥ 0 and radius 3a/2, and point masses μ ≥ 0 at each of the eight positions a(±1,±1,±1). The unknown amplitudes are common to all readings. The origin, axes, geometry, Newtonian G > 0, length a > 0, units and signs are fixed beforehand. The origin, six axial probes at radius a and the probe at (a,a,0) all lie inside the ball's geometric boundary. The last radius is √2 a, and 2 < 9/4 < 3 places the eight corners at √3 a outside the ball. All probes are separate from the point sources.

Write Δg(r) = g(r) − g(0). Inversion pairs cancel the source field at the origin. A uniform additive acceleration makes g(0) equal to that acceleration and cancels in Δg. The first record appends the six signed components Aᵢσ = −σ eᵢ·Δg(σa eᵢ), σ = ±1, to the known setup. The second record exposes B = −eₓ·Δg(a,a,0) from the same preparation and instant. B uses the x component directly. The probes are ideal and have negligible backreaction. The order of disclosure changes only the observer's information.

By spherical symmetry and Newtonian flux, the ball's radial acceleration at interior radius r > 0 is −G(4πρr³/3)/r². Its vector field there is −(4πGρ/3)r⃗, including the continuous value zero at the origin. Thus the ball contributes the same acceleration X = sρ to each of the seven signed scalars, with s = 4πGa/3.

## Field of the eight exterior masses

In units of Gμ/a², the corner sum at coordinate au is the sum over all sign triples v of (v−u)/|v−u|³. This formula specifies a coefficient per unit corner strength and remains valid by multiplication when μ = 0. At u = (1,0,0), the four vₓ = +1 vertices have zero x displacement and their transverse displacements cancel. The four vₓ = −1 vertices each have x displacement −2, distance √6 and cancel transversely. The sum is −C eₓ, with C = 8/(6√6). The source configuration is invariant under every signed axis permutation P, hence its field obeys Δg(Pr) = PΔg(r). All six sites have corner acceleration −σ C(Gμ/a²)eᵢ. With the stated inward sign each contributes the same Y = tμ, where t = CG/a².

At u = (1,1,0), pairing the two z signs for each fixed pair of x and y signs gives the entire vector sum.

The total is −D(eₓ+eᵧ)Gμ/a², with D = 4/(5√5) + 4/27. Exchange of x and y and reflection of z also require equal first two components and zero third. The measured B takes one inward component, giving the corner contribution qY, where

q = D/C = (3/5)√(6/5) + √6/9.

Both terms are positive. Squaring positive quantities gives √(6/5) < 10/9 because 6·81 < 5·100. Also √6 < 3. Thus q < (3/5)(10/9) + 3/9 = 1. The forward equations are Aᵢσ = X+Y and B = X+qY. They give strictly positive signed readings whenever either source amplitude is positive. With both absent, all seven readings vanish.

## Infinitesimal field gradient and the finite trace

Differentiating the field of a mass at R gives the central matrix with entries Gμ[3RᵢRⱼ − |R|²δᵢⱼ]/|R|⁵ for ∂ⱼgᵢ(0). The corners share |R|² = 3a². Summing RᵢRⱼ over all eight vertices gives 8a²δᵢⱼ by cancellation of unlike sign products and equality of the diagonal terms. The entire corner matrix therefore vanishes. Its trace also agrees with Poisson's equation in the corner-free neighbourhood of the origin. Adding the interior ball and uniform field yields

∂ⱼgᵢ(0) = −(4πGρ/3)δᵢⱼ,     −div g(0)/(4πG) = ρ.

Write A for the common axial reading on any compatible record. Centered differences at separations ±a along the three axes give the prescribed finite density report

ρ_fin = (Σᵢ,σ Aᵢσ)/(8πGa) = 6A/(8πGa) = A/s = ρ + (t/s)μ.

This is a function of the existing six observations. It equals the true density exactly when μ = 0 and strictly exceeds it whenever exterior masses are present. ## Exact inverse for the two records

The six-component record is compatible precisely when every entry equals one common A ≥ 0. For A > 0 its full joint parameter set is

(ρ,μ) = (uA/s, (1−u)A/t),     0 ≤ u ≤ 1.

Indeed X,Y ≥ 0 and X+Y = A force this segment, and substitution realizes every point. In particular 0 ≤ ρ ≤ A/s and 0 ≤ μ ≤ A/t are sharp marginal bounds, with the shared u imposing their joint restriction. The endpoints u = 0 and u = 1 give pure corners and pure ball, and the open segment gives strictly mixed sources. A = 0 forces ρ = μ = 0. Thus a positive central density is allowed but never forced by compatible positive axial readings and is impossible for the zero record. Unequal entries or a negative common value admit no preparation.

For an explicit collision fix any A₀ > 0 with acceleration units and the same a, G, apparatus and uniform acceleration. The preparations (A₀/s,0) and (0,A₀/t) both produce six copies of A₀. Their true central densities are A₀/s and zero, and their B readings are A₀ and qA₀. Even the two strictly mixed choices u = 1/3 and 2/3 above give the same positive six-vector at A = A₀ with different positive central densities.

The augmented record is compatible precisely when the axial test holds and qA ≤ B ≤ A. In fact B−qA = (1−q)X and A−B = (1−q)Y. Because 1−q > 0, the unique admissible amplitudes are

ρ = (B−qA)/[s(1−q)],     μ = (A−B)/[t(1−q)].

The two bounds on B make both amplitudes nonnegative, and substitution gives the original record, proving sharpness and sufficiency. A = 0 requires B = 0 and both sources absent. For A > 0, B = qA forces pure corners and zero central density. The interval qA < B < A forces both amplitudes positive. B = A forces pure ball with density A/s. Accordingly every compatible augmented record either forces a unique positive density or forces zero. Any failure of either compatibility test leaves the parameter set empty.
