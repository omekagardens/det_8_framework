## 4. Exact weak-interface obstructions

All witnesses use newborn bits (0,0); their weights are positive. Giving the proof their row identifiers does not give those identifiers to the selector.

### Eligibility from Y: impossible for either bit value

Rows 3 and 15 have the identical input (V,Y=1) and require eligibility 0 and 1. Row 3 has individual marked weight e*f0/4>0; row 15 has h0*(41/44)/4>0. Here e=1/44,f0=1/6,h0=19/30 are the inherited structural constants.

### Actual ordered endpoints: impossible from Y or M, even on eligible inputs

Take the row-15 chain P:a<b<x<y on the fixed address space V={a,b,x,y}. Transport that history by pi=(a y), leaving b,x fixed. The transported chain is P':y<b<x<a. It is an allowed relabelling of the same prepared history with positive inherited weight.

Their ordered source/grounded-return commands are (a,y) and (y,a), which differ in this same address space. Thus neither weak interface selects both actual endpoints. This failure remains even if eligibility and the chain shape are announced for this restricted control; the association with actual handles is still absent.

The analogous diamond relabelling (a y) exchanges its source and return while preserving its Y=0 and M=M_D. Thus neither eligible Y branch provides an escape.

### Covariance and private randomness cannot repair the collisions

Every permutation of V fixes the weak exposed input (V,Y) or (V,M). A deterministic covariant output would therefore have to be fixed by all those permutations. An actual ordered pair of distinct handles cannot have that symmetry. Nor do the chain or diamond's nonconstant pair-type maps. The explicit fixed-roster witnesses exhibit these failures without relying only on this symmetry argument.

More generally let K(.|i) be any history-independent conditional output law produced using private randomness from permitted input i. Probability-one exact success on a realization requiring component o forces K(o|i)=1. A second realization with the same i and required o'!=o forces K(o'|i)=1. The two requirements contradict normalization. Apply this to each collision above. It covers stochastic eligibility, stochastic endpoints and stochastic pair maps, separately, and therefore the complete weak commands as well. All witness histories have positive probability; success-only deletion or reproducing a marginal signal law is not this pointwise criterion.

In particular the multiset's successful gate does not cure either of its subsequent addressing failures. Conversely, the strong diamond's middle tie causes no conflict because every allowed swap fixes its whole command, as proved above.

The completed-order VIEW is dispensable for selecting the accepted model's commands on THIS prepared support, once the strong terminal interface is explicitly supplied. 1. Observed data: the prepared classical descendant counters, with the chosen level of terminal read permission.
