## 2. Interior cavity stress shift and the No-Passive-Wall-Stress Obstruction

Inside $\Omega$, retain the Weyl-ordered minimally coupled scalar stress-energy tensor $T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm sc}:=\kappa\bigl[\frac12\{\partial^\mu\Phi_{\rm sc},\partial^\nu\Phi_{\rm sc}\}-\frac12\eta^{\mu\nu}Z\bigr]$ ($Z:=-c^{-2}\dot\Phi_{\rm sc}^2+|\nabla\Phi_{\rm sc}|^2$) on $\mathcal S(\mathbb R^{12})$. In any Hermite basis state $|\ell\rangle$, $X_m(t)Y_m(t)+Y_m(t)X_m(t)=(\cos^2\omega_mt-\sin^2\omega_mt)\{Q_m,P_m\}+2\cos\omega_mt\sin\omega_mt(P_m^2-Q_m^2)$ has expectation $0+2\cos\omega_mt\sin\omega_mt\bigl((\ell_m+\frac12)-(\ell_m+\frac12)\bigr)=0$, while $\langle\ell,X_m(t)^2\ell\rangle=\langle\ell,Y_m(t)^2\ell\rangle=\ell_m+\frac12$. Summing against $p^{(i)}(\ell)$ and using $\frac{\kappa}{4}B_m^2k_m^2=\frac{A_*\omega_m}{2V}=:\rho_m>0$ gives for every $x\in\Omega$ and $i\in\{0,1\}$:
$$
\rho_m^{(i)}:=\bigl(2\nu_m^{(i)}+1\bigr)\rho_m=\left(\nu_m^{(i)}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{A_*\omega_m}{V}>0,\qquad
\rho_{\rm cav}^{(i)}:=\sum_{m=0}^{11}\rho_m^{(i)}=\frac{E_{\rm cav}^{(i)}}{V}>0,
$$
$$
\Delta\rho_m:=2\Delta\nu_m\rho_m=\Delta\nu_m\frac{A_*\omega_m}{V},\qquad
\Delta\rho_{\rm cav}:=\rho_{\rm cav}^{(1)}-\rho_{\rm cav}^{(0)}=\sum_{m=0}^{11}\Delta\rho_m=\frac{\Delta E_{\rm cav}}{V},
$$
$$
\boxed{\omega^{(i)}\bigl(T^{00}_{\rm sc}(x)\bigr)=\rho_{\rm cav}^{(i)},\quad
\omega^{(i)}\bigl(T^{0a}_{\rm sc}(x)\bigr)=0,\quad
\omega^{(i)}\bigl(T^{ab}_{\rm sc}(x)\bigr)
=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\rho_{\rm cav}^{(i)},\,\sum_{m=0}^{11}\rho_m^{(i)}\cos 2\alpha_m(x_1),\,\sum_{m=0}^{11}\rho_m^{(i)}\cos 2\alpha_m(x_1)\right).}
\tag{7}
$$
Indeed, $\omega^{(i)}(c^{-2}\dot\Phi_{\rm sc}^2)=\frac{2}{\kappa}\sum_{m=0}^{11}\rho_m^{(i)}\cos^2\alpha_m(x_1)$, $\omega^{(i)}(\frac12\{\partial_a\Phi_{\rm sc},\partial_b\Phi_{\rm sc}\})=\delta_{a1}\delta_{b1}\frac{2}{\kappa}\sum_{m=0}^{11}\rho_m^{(i)}\sin^2\alpha_m(x_1)$, and $\omega^{(i)}(Z)=-\frac{2}{\kappa}\sum_{m=0}^{11}\rho_m^{(i)}\cos 2\alpha_m(x_1)$, so $\omega^{(i)}(T^{00}_{\rm sc})=\sum_{m=0}^{11}\rho_m^{(i)}(\cos^2\alpha_m+\sin^2\alpha_m)=\rho_{\rm cav}^{(i)}$, $\omega^{(i)}(T^{11}_{\rm sc})=\sum_{m=0}^{11}\rho_m^{(i)}(2\sin^2\alpha_m+\cos 2\alpha_m)=\rho_{\rm cav}^{(i)}$, and $\omega^{(i)}(T^{22}_{\rm sc})=\omega^{(i)}(T^{33}_{\rm sc})=-\frac\kappa2\omega^{(i)}(Z)=\sum_{m=0}^{11}\rho_m^{(i)}\cos 2\alpha_m(x_1)$. Inside $\operatorname{int}(\Omega)$, $\partial_\mu\omega^{(i)}(T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm sc}(x))=0$ holds identically.

Can one hold the exterior confining collar **passive (unadjusted)** across $i\in\{0,1\}$ so that only the quantum cavity state changes? Theorem 2 proves this is mathematically impossible in linearized gravity.

**Theorem 2 (No-Passive-Wall-Stress Obstruction to Source-Coupled Linearized Gravity).** *Let $T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm ext}\in L^1_{\rm loc}(\mathbb R^3)$ be any static exterior stress-energy tensor supported in $\mathbb R^3\setminus\operatorname{int}(\Omega)$ such that $T^{\mu\nu,(0)}_{\rm tot}:=\mathbf 1_\Omega\,\omega^{(0)}(T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm sc})+\mathbf 1_{\mathbb R^3\setminus\Omega}\,T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm ext}$ satisfies $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu,(0)}_{\rm tot}=0$ in $\mathcal D'(\mathbb R^3)$. Hold $T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm ext}$ unadjusted when the cavity is prepared in state $\omega^{(1)}$ with occupation difference $\Delta\nu=\nu^{(1)}-\nu^{(0)}\in\mathbb R^{12}$, forming the hybrid whole-space tensor $T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm hyb}:=\mathbf 1_\Omega\,\omega^{(1)}(T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm sc})+\mathbf 1_{\mathbb R^3\setminus\Omega}\,T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm ext}$, and set $\Delta T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm sc}:=\omega^{(1)}(T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm sc})-\omega^{(0)}(T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm sc})$ and $\Delta P_\perp(x_1):=\sum_{m=0}^{11}\Delta\rho_m\cos 2\alpha_m(x_1)$. Then:*
1. *In $\mathcal D'(\mathbb R^3)$, $\partial_0T^{0\nu}_{\rm hyb}=\partial_aT^{a0}_{\rm hyb}=0$, while the spatial divergence is concentrated on the six faces of $\partial\Omega$:*
   $$
   \partial_aT^{ab}_{\rm hyb}=-n_a\Delta T^{ab}_{\rm sc}\,\delta_{\partial\Omega},\qquad
   \partial_aT^{a1}_{\rm hyb}=\Delta\rho_{\rm cav}\left[\delta\!\left(x_1+\frac{L}{2}\right)-\delta\!\left(x_1-\frac{L}{2}\right)\right]\mathbf 1_{[-L/2,L/2]^2}(x_2,x_3),
   $$
   $$
   \partial_aT^{a2}_{\rm hyb}=\Delta P_\perp(x_1)\left[\delta\!\left(x_2+\frac{L}{2}\right)-\delta\!\left(x_2-\frac{L}{2}\right)\right]\mathbf 1_{[-L/2,L/2]^2}(x_1,x_3),
   $$
   $$
   \partial_aT^{a3}_{\rm hyb}=\Delta P_\perp(x_1)\left[\delta\!\left(x_3+\frac{L}{2}\right)-\delta\!\left(x_3-\frac{L}{2}\right)\right]\mathbf 1_{[-L/2,L/2]^2}(x_1,x_2).
   \tag{8}
   $$
2. *For any smooth cutoff $\theta_{\rm cut}\in C_c^\infty(B_{2L}(0))$ equal to $1$ on a neighborhood of $\overline\Omega$, testing against $f(x)=\theta_{\rm cut}(x)(x_1,0,0)$ yields $\langle\partial_aT^{ab}_{\rm hyb},f_b\rangle=-\Delta E_{\rm cav}$. Moreover, $\partial_aT^{ab}_{\rm hyb}=0$ in $\mathcal D'(\mathbb R^3)$ **if and only if** $\Delta\rho_m=0$ (equivalently $\Delta\nu_m=0$) for every $m\in\{0,1,\ldots,11\}$—including for equal-total-energy spectral swaps where $\Delta E_{\rm cav}=0$ with signed $\Delta\nu_m$.*
3. *Consequently, whenever $\Delta\nu\ne 0$ (and in particular whenever $\Delta E_{\rm cav}>0$), **no** static distribution $\bar h_{\mu\nu}\in\mathcal D'(\mathbb R^3)$ (decaying at infinity or otherwise) can simultaneously solve $\nabla^2\bar h_{\mu\nu}=-(16\pi G/c^4)T^{\rm hyb}_{\mu\nu}$ and satisfy the harmonic gauge condition $\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu}=0$ on $\mathbb R^3$:*
   $$
   \langle\partial_aT^{ab}_{\rm hyb},f_b\rangle=-\Delta E_{\rm cav},\qquad
   \partial_aT^{ab}_{\rm hyb}=0\iff\Delta\nu=0,\qquad
   \nabla^2\bigl(\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu b}\bigr)=-\frac{16\pi G}{c^4}\partial_aT^{ab}_{\rm hyb}\ne 0\quad(\Delta\nu\ne 0).
   \tag{9}
   $$

*Proof.* (1) Subtracting $T^{\mu\nu,(0)}_{\rm tot}$ gives $T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm hyb}=T^{\mu\nu,(0)}_{\rm tot}+\mathbf 1_\Omega\,\Delta T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm sc}$. Because $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu,(0)}_{\rm tot}=0$ in $\mathcal D'(\mathbb R^3)$, $\Delta T^{0a}_{\rm sc}=0$, and $\Delta T^{ab}_{\rm sc}(x)=\operatorname{diag}(\Delta\rho_{\rm cav},\Delta P_\perp(x_1),\Delta P_\perp(x_1))$ is smooth on $\mathbb R^3$ with $\partial_a\Delta T^{ab}_{\rm sc}=0$ everywhere, the divergence theorem on $\Omega$ yields for every test field $h\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb R^3)$:
$$
\langle\partial_aT^{ab}_{\rm hyb},h_b\rangle
=\langle\partial_a(\mathbf 1_\Omega\Delta T^{ab}_{\rm sc}),h_b\rangle
=-\int_\Omega\Delta T^{ab}_{\rm sc}(x)\,\partial_ah_b(x)\,d^3x

$$
Evaluating $-n_a\Delta T^{ab}_{\rm sc}$ on the outward normals $n=\pm e_1$ at $x_1=\pm L/2$ ($\mp\Delta\rho_{\rm cav}$), $n=\pm e_2$ at $x_2=\pm L/2$ ($\mp\Delta P_\perp(x_1)$), and $n=\pm e_3$ at $x_3=\pm L/2$ ($\mp\Delta P_\perp(x_1)$) establishes (8).

(2) For $f(x)=\theta_{\rm cut}(x)(x_1,0,0)$ with $\theta_{\rm cut}\equiv 1$ near $\overline\Omega$, $\partial_1f_1=1$ on $\Omega$ and $f_2=f_3=0$, so $\langle\partial_aT^{ab}_{\rm hyb},f_b\rangle=-\int_\Omega\Delta\rho_{\rm cav}\,d^3x=-\Delta\rho_{\rm cav}V=-\Delta E_{\rm cav}$ (equivalently $\Delta\rho_{\rm cav}L^2[(-L/2)-(+L/2)]=-\Delta\rho_{\rm cav}L^3=-\Delta E_{\rm cav}$ from (8)). If $\Delta\nu=0$, then $\Delta\rho_m=0$ for all $m$, so $\Delta T^{ab}_{\rm sc}=0$ and $\partial_aT^{ab}_{\rm hyb}=0$. Conversely, suppose $\partial_aT^{ab}_{\rm hyb}=0$ in $\mathcal D'(\mathbb R^3)$, even when $\Delta E_{\rm cav}=0$. Pick $\psi\in C_c^\infty((-L/2,L/2))$ with $\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}\psi(x_3)\,dx_3=1$ (by normalizing $b((L/4)^2-x_3^2)$, supported in $[-L/4,L/4]\subset(-L/2,L/2)$) and $\zeta\in C_c^\infty((0,L))$ with $\zeta(L/2)=1$ (e.g., $\zeta(x_2):=b((L/4)^2-(x_2-L/2)^2)/b((L/4)^2)$, supported in $[L/4,3L/4]\subset(0,L)$ so $\zeta(-L/2)=0$, isolating the single face $x_2=+L/2$). $$

=-\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}\Delta P_\perp(x_1)\phi(x_1)\,dx_1\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}\psi(x_3)\,dx_3
=-\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}\Delta P_\perp(x_1)\phi(x_1)\,dx_1.
$$
If $\Delta P_\perp(x_0)\ne 0$ for some $x_0\in(-L/2,L/2)$, continuity gives $|\Delta P_\perp(x_1)|\ge\frac12|\Delta P_\perp(x_0)|>0$ with constant sign on $[x_0-\delta,x_0+\delta]\subset(-L/2,L/2)$, and testing against a nonnegative bump $\phi\in C_c^\infty((x_0-\delta,x_0+\delta))$ with $\int\phi=1$ yields a nonzero integral, a contradiction; hence $\Delta P_\perp(x_1)=0$ on $(-L/2,L/2)$ and by continuity on $[-L/2,L/2]$. Changing variables to $s=\frac\pi L(x_1+L/2)\in[0,\pi]$ ($dx_1=\frac L\pi\,ds$, $2\alpha_m(x_1)=2n_ms$ with pairwise distinct positive integers $2n_m=2(47-2m)\in\{50,54,\ldots,94\}$), $2\cos(2n_ms)\cos(2n_{m'}s)=\cos(2(n_m-n_{m'})s)+\cos(2(n_m+n_{m'})s)$ integrates over $[0,\pi]$ to $\pi\delta_{mm'}$, giving $\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}\cos 2\alpha_m(x_1)\cos 2\alpha_{m'}(x_1)\,dx_1=\frac L2\delta_{mm'}$. Integrating $\Delta P_\perp(x_1)\cos 2\alpha_{m'}(x_1)=0$ over $[-L/2,L/2]$ therefore forces $\frac L2\Delta\rho_{m'}=0$, so $\Delta\rho_{m'}=0$ and (since $\rho_{m'}>0$) $\Delta\nu_{m'}=0$ for every $m'\in\{0,\ldots,11\}$.

(3) Suppose $\bar h_{\mu\nu}\in\mathcal D'(\mathbb R^3)$ is static ($\partial_0\bar h_{\mu\nu}=0$) and satisfies $\nabla^2\bar h_{\mu\nu}=-(16\pi G/c^4)T^{\rm hyb}_{\mu\nu}$ in $\mathcal D'(\mathbb R^3)$. If $\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu}=0$ in $\mathcal D'(\mathbb R^3)$, then $\partial_aT^{ab}_{\rm hyb}=0$ in $\mathcal D'(\mathbb R^3)$, forcing $\Delta\nu=0$ by part (2). Physical wall-stress dressing in the exterior collar is therefore mathematically mandatory whenever $\Delta\nu\ne 0$. $\square$

## 3. Universal spectrum-independent dominant-energy bound and common-casing Beltrami collar family

By Theorem 2, whenever the prepared cavity occupation spectrum changes ($\Delta\nu\ne 0$), the exterior confining collar must dress its spatial stresses to maintain $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu,(i)}_{\rm tot}=0$. In the vacuum-only [all-branch cavity–collar construction](all_branch_cavity_collar_source_for_four_birth_fields.md), the dominant-energy bound on the stress-cutoff shell $\frac32L\le|x|\le 2L$ exploited the vacuum proportionality $\rho_m\propto k_m$ so that $\frac{\rho_m}{2k_m}$ was independent of $m$. For an arbitrary excited spectrum $(\nu_0^{(i)},\ldots,\nu_{11}^{(i)})\in[0,\infty)^{12}$, $\rho_m^{(i)}=(2\nu_m^{(i)}+1)\rho_m$ is no longer proportional to $k_m$. We now prove from first principles that the **exact same universal constant** $M_{\rm st}^{(12)}$ governs **every** nonnegative 12-mode spectrum $\nu^{(i)}\in[0,\infty)^{12}$.

For each $i\in\{0,1\}$, define on all $\mathbb R^3$ the smooth diagonal interior Beltrami stress potential $\Phi^{\mathrm{int},(i)}(x)=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(0,\Phi_2^{\mathrm{int},(i)}(x),\Phi_3^{\mathrm{int},(i)}(x)\bigr)$ and smooth radial cutoffs ($b(t):=e^{-1/t}$ for $t>0$ and $0$ for $t\le 0$, $s:=|x|/L$, $\sigma:=s^2=|x|^2/L^2$) retained from the [compact four-chain cavity source](compact_cavity_source_for_four_chain_field.md) and [all-branch cavity–collar construction](all_branch_cavity_collar_source_for_four_birth_fields.md):

$$

$$

$$

\tag{10}

Because $\overline\Omega=[-L/2,L/2]^3\subset\overline{B_{\frac{\sqrt3}{2}L}(0)}\subset B_L(0)\subset B_{3L/2}(0)\subset B_{2L}(0)\subset B_{3L}(0)$ and $\sigma=|x|^2/L^2$ is a smooth polynomial on $\mathbb R^3$ with cutoff denominators bounded below by $e^{-8/7}$, $e^{-8/5}$, and $e^{-2/5}$, we have $\Phi^{(i)}_{mn}\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$ ($\operatorname{supp}\Phi^{(i)}_{mn}\subseteq\overline{B_{2L}(0)}$) and $T^{\mu\nu,(i)}_{\rm tot}\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$ ($\operatorname{supp}T^{\mu\nu,(i)}_{\rm tot}\subseteq\overline{B_{3L}(0)}$). Moreover, $2\alpha_m(-x_1)=2(47-2m)\pi-2\alpha_m(x_1)$ gives $g^{(i)}(-x_1)=g^{(i)}(x_1)$, $\Phi^{(i)}_{mn}(-x)=\Phi^{(i)}_{mn}(x)$, and $\Phi_2^{\mathrm{int},(i)}(x_1,x_2)=\Phi_3^{\mathrm{int},(i)}(x_1,x_2)$.

**Theorem 3 (Spectrum-independent universal dominant-energy constant $M_{\rm st}^{(12)}$ and admissible collar family).** *For **every** nonnegative 12-mode occupation spectrum $\nu^{(i)}=(\nu_0^{(i)},\ldots,\nu_{11}^{(i)})\in[0,\infty)^{12}$ in (3):*

   $$
   $$
   \sum_{m=1}^{3}\bigl|\Phi_m^{\mathrm{int},(i)}(x)\bigr|<\frac{9}{8}\rho_{\rm cav}^{(i)}L^2,\qquad

   $$
   M_{\rm st}^{(12)}:=1+57e^{-6/7}+576e^{-12/7}+972e^{-13/7}+4608e^{-20/7}>1.
   $$

   $$
   \partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu,(i)}_{\rm tot}=0,\qquad
   \int_{\mathbb R^3}T^{ab,(i)}_{\rm tot}(x)\,d^3x=0,\qquad
   \int_{\mathbb R^3\setminus\Omega}T^{ab,(i)}_{\rm tot}(x)\,d^3x=-\operatorname{diag}\bigl(E_{\rm cav}^{(i)},0,0\bigr),
   $$
   $$
   \boxed{\bigl\|T^{ab,(i)}_{\rm tot}(x)\bigr\|\le T^{00,(i)}_{\rm tot}(x)\qquad\forall\,x\in\mathbb R^3.}
   \tag{11}
   $$

*Proof.* (1) Every mode index $m\in\{0,1,\ldots,11\}$ satisfies $47-2m\ge 25$, so $k_m=\frac{(47-2m)\pi}{L}\ge k_{11}=\frac{25\pi}{L}>0$. \le\frac{1}{k_{11}^2}\sum_{m=0}^{11}\rho_m^{(i)}=\frac{\rho_{\rm cav}^{(i)}L^2}{625\pi^2}<\frac{1}{8}\rho_{\rm cav}^{(i)}L^2,
