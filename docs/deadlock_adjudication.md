# DET v8.0 — Deadlock Adjudication

**Does DET resolve the four deadlocks *better* than Copenhagen, Many-Worlds, QBism, and Bohm? This is argued, not measured — and it is the real contest, because DET's novelty is the description, not the physics.**

## The criteria

| Criterion | What it asks |
|---|---|
| **Coherence** | Does the resolution avoid internal contradiction? |
| **Parsimony** | Does it add the fewest new entities/commitments? |
| **Observable-anchoring** | Is it anchored to observables, or to another interpretation? |
| **Completeness** | Does it resolve *all four* deadlocks, or only one? |
| **Fruitfulness** | Does it generate constraints/questions (fruit-first)? |

---

## Deadlock 1 — Time (Block Universe vs. Presentism)

| Position | What it asserts | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Block universe (eternalism)** | all events exist; the present is an illusion | coherent with relativity; but the *experienced* present is unexplained, and "illusion" is itself an interpretation of the observable causal structure |
| **Naive presentism** | only the present exists | contradicts relativity (no frame-independent "now") |
| **Growing block (Ellis 2014)** | past + present exist, future doesn't | relativistic (J⁻(e) invariant), but "the growing edge" is still an interpretation |
| **DET (record growth)** | the present is the *commit event*; the record is the trace; time is irreversible record growth | **anchored to `≺` + record + irreversibility (observables); the "only the present IS" is Status M, flagged** |

**Adjudication:** DET is a *growing-block* variant — a respectable, contested position — with one concrete advantage: its "record growth" is anchored to an *observable asymmetry* (the record only ever grows, never shrinks), so the ontology is tied to irreversibility rather than floated free. It does **not** beat eternalism on purely empirical grounds (they are observationally equivalent); it beats it on *explanatory* grounds (the present is real, not an illusion). **Wins on coherence + anchoring; ties on empirics.**

## Deadlock 2 — Quantum (the Measurement Problem)

| Position | What it asserts | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Copenhagen** | collapse, but refuses to say what it is | the "shut up and calculate" dodge — coherence by silence |
| **Many-Worlds (Everett)** | no collapse; all branches real | reifies unobserved branches (ontological explosion); the Born rule is *derived* only with difficulty |
| **Bohm** | hidden trajectories under a pilot wave | trajectories are unobservable by design; hides the problem |
| **QBism** | states are *degrees of belief* | gives up realism; "collapse" = updating credences; no ontology of the system |
| **DET** | superposition = open relational constraint; **commit = fact genesis** (a primitive, not a process) | **collapse is *replaced* by commit; the observable (definite outcome) is the anchor** |

**Adjudication:** DET's move is genuinely distinct — it does **not** add a collapse mechanism (Copenhagen's problem) nor deny collapse (Many-Worlds's reification). It makes *commit* a primitive, the same move DET makes for time (the present is primitive). This is coherent and anchored to the definite outcome. **But honestly:** making commit primitive does not explain the Born-rule *probabilities* — *why this outcome with weight μ(i)* — that is O1, and T2a showed grade-2 is an *empirical* choice, not a theorem. So DET dissolves "collapse is mysterious" but does **not** dissolve "why these probabilities." **Wins on coherence + anchoring; the Born rule stays open (shared with the field).**

## Deadlock 3 — Agency (Free Will vs. Physics)

| Position | What it asserts | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Epiphenomenalism** | agency is an illusion | coherent with physics, but denies the very thing at issue |
| **Dualism** | agency is a ghost force | violates conservation laws / closure |
| **Compatibilism** | agency = the deterministic process itself | redefines agency into something unrecognizable |
| **DET** | agency = *present enactment* (Status M, quarantined) | **no agency variable ever enters an equation ("no God in the equations")** |

**Adjudication:** DET's position is the *inverse* of the god-of-the-gaps move — it removes agency from physics entirely rather than inserting it. That is a genuine methodological advance. **But it is Status M:** no unique discriminator distinguishes "present enactment" from "primitive stochastic occurrence." So DET is coherent, parsimonious, and honest — but it does **not** *establish* agency, it *protects* it from being diluted. **Wins on coherence + parsimony; unfalsifiable as an object (acknowledged).**

## Deadlock 4 — History (Fixed Past vs. Healing)

| Position | What it asserts | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Retrocausality** | the past can be changed | violates the causal order the rest of physics uses |
| **Fixed past** | the past is immutable; healing is subjective | denies the phenomenology of recovery outright |
| **DET** | the past *event* is fixed; its *structural carrying* κ is mutable | **anchored to κ-recovery (observable); the "mutable carrying" reading is Status M** |

**Adjudication:** DET separates *what happened* (fixed) from *what it weighs on the present* (mutable), and anchors the latter to the observable recovery kinetics. This is the most clearly *novel and coherent* of the four — it dissolves the retrocausality-vs-fixed-past dilemma without denying either the fixity of events or the reality of recovery. **Wins on coherence + anchoring + fruitfulness (it generated the κ program).**

## Overall verdict

| Position | Deadlocks resolved | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Copenhagen | quantum (badly) | collapse unexplained |
| Many-Worlds | quantum | reifies branches; silent on agency/history |
| QBism | quantum | gives up realism |
| Bohm | quantum | hidden variables |
| **DET** | **all four** | the ontology is largely Status M; the Born rule stays open |

**DET's claim to strength is *unification*:** one framework, four deadlocks, a *single* primitive family (record / commit / present), anchored to observables (`≺`, `X_e`, `I₂`, κ-recovery), with the interpretive periphery honestly flagged Status M rather than smuggled in.

**Its honest weakness:** the four resolutions are *interpretations* — they are adjudicated by coherence and fruit, not by experiment. The one place DET could have been empirically novel (κ as a field) is expected null. And the Born rule remains an open, empirical choice (grade-2). This is not a failure of the ontology; it is the honest boundary of what an ontology *can* claim without sneaking in interpretations.

**The criterion for DET to win the contest is therefore:** does one framework resolving all four deadlocks — coherently, parsimoniously, and observably-anchored — beat four separate frameworks that each resolve only one? That is the question DET puts to the field, and it is argued, not measured.
