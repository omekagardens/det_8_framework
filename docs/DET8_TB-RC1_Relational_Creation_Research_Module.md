## 0. Purpose

This module gathers a developing DET8 line of thought:

1. Only the present moment exists.
2. Present existence is necessarily relational participation.
3. Anything that presently affects or is affected by another participant must possess at least a capacity for relation.
4. Coherent relations form nested regimes: constituents, organs, organisms, communities, ecosystems, and potentially creation as a regime-of-regimes.
5. A larger regime can possess an irreducible unity and recognizable relational character without erasing its constituent regimes.
6. Theology may therefore investigate whether relational creation participates in, or in a strong sense is, the Body of Christ.
7. The Fall can be interpreted as distortion of relation rather than loss of relation.
8. Redemption can be interpreted as the healing and right ordering of an embodied creation rather than extraction from disposable matter.
9. DET cannot directly quantify God, love, consciousness, Christ, salvation, or the interior of presence. It can, however, search for the **fruit** and **prints** of different relational organizations.

The purpose is not to insert theology into physical equations. The purpose is to establish a disciplined research grammar in which theological hypotheses can guide questions, while measurable traces remain governed by ordinary causal, energetic, and falsification requirements.

---

## 1. Working Thesis

> **All present existence participates relationally. Relational capacity is more primitive than biological life, agency, selfhood, or consciousness. Coherent relations form nested regimes. Human persons are selfhood-bearing regimes nested within bodily, social, ecological, and cosmic creation. As a theological hypothesis, all relational creation may participate in the Body of Christ, whose personal unity is grounded in God, who is love. The Fall is not departure from relation but its distortion; redemption is the healing of relational order so that creation increasingly bears the fruit of its Christic identity. Matter is therefore not disposable scaffolding. Redemption must transform embodied creation rather than merely extract selves from it.**

This thesis contains several claims of different strength. They must not be treated as one proven package.

## 3. Core Distinctions

The word **life** is too compressed to carry the entire argument. This module therefore distinguishes several levels.


The distinctions can be expressed schematically:

\[
\boxed{
\text{relational capacity}
\neq
\text{biological life}
\neq
\text{agency}
\neq
\text{selfhood}
\neq
\text{consciousness}
\neq
\text{personhood}
}
\]

### 3.1 Relational capacity

**Relational capacity** is the minimum capacity to participate in a present interaction: to affect, be affected, constrain, receive constraint, transmit change, or enter a bond.

It does **not** imply that the participant:

- is biologically alive;
- has subjective experience;
- chooses;
- possesses a self;
- has intentions;
- is morally responsible.

A stone supporting a foot, air carrying speech, a cell responding to a chemical gradient, and a person answering a question all participate relationally, but not necessarily in the same manner or at the same level.

### 3.2 Biological life

Biological life is a rich organization of relational capacities involving metabolism, self-maintenance, reproduction, adaptation, repair, and boundary-mediated exchange. It is not the beginning of relation; it is one highly organized expression of relation.

### 3.3 Agency

Agency remains a selfhood-bearing regime’s present enactment, not a stored coefficient or physical force. A measurable disposition, readiness signal, habit, or preference may be a record-side proxy, but none is identical to agency.

### 3.4 Consciousness

Consciousness remains Track B. Greater complexity, integration, information flow, or regime size does not by itself prove consciousness.

### 3.5 Personality

A regime’s **personality print** can provisionally mean a stable yet context-sensitive style of whole-regime response: recognizable orientation, characteristic repair behavior, relation-specific adaptation, or continuity of response across changing constituents.

A personality print is not proof of subjective consciousness. It is an observable pattern that may motivate—but cannot settle—the ontological question.

## 4. From Relations to Regimes

### 4.1 Relational priority

The ordinary picture is:

\[
\text{independent objects} \rightarrow \text{relations between them}.
\]

The proposed DET8 Track B reversal is:

\[
\text{present relations}
\rightarrow
\text{coherence}
\rightarrow
\text{regime}
\rightarrow
\text{stable identity or selfhood}.
\]

This does not require denying participants. It asks whether participants and relations are co-constitutive, with stable “things” emerging as coherent relational organizations.

### 4.2 Provisional regime definition

A **regime** is not merely a set of nearby nodes. It is a differentiated relational organization in which:

1. internal participants constrain and enable one another;
2. the organization has some persistence across constituent turnover;
3. internal differentiation contributes to whole-regime function;
4. boundaries regulate rather than eliminate exchange;
5. perturbations can propagate across the organization;
6. the whole can exhibit repair, adaptation, or failure modes not attributable to one constituent alone;
7. participation in the whole changes the possibilities available to each part.

### 4.3 Body relation

A **body relation** is stronger than membership in a collection. A participant is bodily related to a regime when there is:

- mutual dependence;
- bidirectional constraint;
- shared vulnerability;
- differentiated contribution;
- resource or information exchange;
- whole-to-part and part-to-whole effects;
- some continuity of the whole across local change.

This allows the following nesting:

\[
\text{constituents}
\rightarrow
\text{cells}
\rightarrow
\text{organs}
\rightarrow
\text{organism}
\rightarrow
\text{community}
\rightarrow
\text{ecosystem}
\rightarrow
\text{creation}.
\]

The arrows do not mean that every level is equally integrated, equally conscious, or bounded in the same way.

### 4.4 Differentiated unity

Healthy unity is not homogeneity.

A coherent body preserves the reality of its parts. A heart does not become a lung. A person does not become an interchangeable unit of a community. A species does not lose its distinctiveness by participating in an ecosystem.

This yields a central constraint:

> **A healthy higher regime increases meaningful participation without erasing lower-regime identity.**

Maximum integration is therefore not the goal. Pathological over-integration, centralized control, coercive synchronization, and suppression of local freedom may destroy the very differentiated life that makes a regime a body.

## 5. Creation as a Regime-of-Regimes

### 5.1 The upward application of regime ontology

DET commonly considers lower-level structures composing larger regimes. The present proposal extends the question upward:

> If a human person is an irreducible regime composed of many living sub-regimes, can creation itself possess an irreducible whole-regime unity?

The fact that a person is not reducible to a list of cells provides an analogy, not a proof. Nevertheless, it opens a legitimate Track B question: whether creation has a whole-level identity, character, or interiority that is not captured by listing its components.

### 5.2 The cosmic personality question

The strong hypothesis is not merely that creation is a network. It is that the total relational regime may possess a deep personal unity.

A possible theological identification is:

\[
\boxed{	ext{Christ as the personal unity of relational creation}}
\]

This must not be read as a physical derivation. Regime size does not entail consciousness, and consciousness does not entail Christ.

### 5.3 Christ must not be treated as an emergent byproduct

Three models must be distinguished:

| Model | Claim | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **Emergent cosmic mind** | Enough relations eventually generate a cosmic person called Christ | Weak fit with the Christian claim that creation is through and in Christ; risks making God dependent on the completed universe |
| **Identity collapse** | The sum of all physical things simply is God | Collapses Creator and creation; makes evil or damaged relation identical with God |
| **Participatory Christic regime** | Creation exists through, in, and toward a personal relational unity grounded in Christ; the body participates in Christ rather than producing Christ | Preferred working hypothesis for study |

The preferred model is therefore neither a distant external deity nor a cosmic mind mechanically produced from below.

### 5.4 No global-state smuggling

DET governance prohibits a universal physical state or scheduler from being treated as the real present. Therefore, “creation as one body” cannot mean a simultaneously accessible global data structure.

The creation regime must instead be conceived as:

- distributed;
- locally enacted;
- composed through causal and relational continuity;
- not centrally clocked;
- not reducible to a universal observer;
- not represented by the simulation scheduler.

The theological whole may be real without being a physically sampled global time-slice.

### 5.5 The Open Whole Problem

Ordinary regimes have boundaries and environments. Creation, considered as the physical whole, has no external physical environment.

**Open problem RC-O1:** Can a regime-of-regimes possess coherence without an external physical boundary?

Possible approaches include:

- coherence through universal internal relation rather than outside exchange;
- self-differentiating internal boundaries;
- closure under causal participation rather than spatial enclosure;
- a theological distinction between creation’s physical totality and God as its non-object-like ground, not as an external node.

No solution is assumed here.

## 6. “God Is Love” as an Ontological Claim

The theological phrase **God is love** can be read more strongly than “God often behaves lovingly.” Love is intrinsically relational: giving, receiving, knowing, sustaining, responding, making room, repairing, and seeking another’s good.

Under relational ontology, the phrase may suggest:

> God is not one more node inside the relational fabric. God is the personal source, sustainer, and right-ordering fullness through whom relation exists and becomes life-giving.

Several guardrails follow.

### 6.1 God is not every relation as such

Abuse, domination, exploitation, and deceit are relational, but they are not therefore God. Relation is ontologically unavoidable; its ordering can be life-giving or destructive.

\[
\boxed{	ext{relation exists} \neq \text{relation is rightly ordered}}
\]

### 6.2 Love is not a physical field

DET must not introduce `love_strength`, `christ_field`, `grace_force`, or a divine adjustment term. Love can orient interpretation and generate research questions, but all physical models must use explicit causal channels and ordinary accounting.

### 6.3 Love as healthy coherence across boundaries

A provisional Track B formulation is:

> **Love is the free, truthful, life-giving ordering of relations in which differentiated participants are enabled to flourish together.**

This is not a complete definition. It is a research criterion that emphasizes:

- reciprocity without strict transaction;
- freedom rather than coercion;
- care for vulnerable members;
- truthful feedback;
- repair after harm;
- preservation of difference;
- willingness to bear cost for another;
- whole-regime fruit without sacrificing the least.

## 7. The Body of Christ: A Hypothesis Ladder

The phrase **Body of Christ** should be studied through several progressively stronger hypotheses.

| Level | Hypothesis | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **BC-1 Ecclesial body** | The community consciously following Christ is the Body of Christ | Traditional interpretive baseline |
| **BC-2 Firstfruits body** | The church is an intentionally responsive or healing sub-regime within a wider creation already held in Christ | T/M hypothesis |
| **BC-3 Participatory creation** | All creation participates in the life, sustaining relation, and reconciling purpose of Christ | T/M hypothesis |
| **BC-4 Strong body hypothesis** | All relational creation is already, ontologically, the Body of Christ; the Fall distorts its expression and redemption heals it | Strong working hypothesis |
| **BC-5 Emergent-Christ model** | The totality of relations produces Christ as an emergent cosmic mind | Not preferred; retained as an adversarial contrast |

### 7.1 Preferred provisional formulation

> **All relational creation may already belong to and participate in the Body of Christ. The consciously faithful community is not necessarily the entirety of that body, but may be a sub-regime called to recognize, serve, repair, and truthfully express the body’s deeper identity.**

This preserves the special vocation of discipleship without making the rest of creation disposable or ontologically outside Christ.

### 7.2 “I do not need you” as ontological denial

Within a nested relational body, the statement “I do not need you” becomes more than social arrogance. It attempts to deny the dependence by which the speaker exists.

The denial can take several forms:

- person against person;
- class against class;
- humanity against other species;
- economy against soil and water;
- spirituality against embodiment;
- heaven against Earth;
- church against the creation it claims God will discard.

The statement may be false even when the ignored participant appears weak, peripheral, or replaceable. Low visibility or low centrality does not equal lack of contribution.

### 7.3 Non-disposability is not frozen permanence

A body changes. Cells die, organisms metabolize, ecosystems transform, and matter is recycled. Therefore, non-disposability cannot mean that every present configuration must remain forever.

It means:

- no participant is ontologically mere waste;
- no being’s value is exhausted by its utility to humans;
- transformation should not be confused with abandonment;
- death or pruning must not become an excuse for domination;
- the cost imposed on vulnerable participants remains morally and relationally significant.

## 8. The Fall as Relational Pathology

If relation is fundamental, the Fall cannot mean that creation ceased to be relational. It can mean that relation became distorted.

\[
\boxed{	ext{Fall} = \text{relation retained} + \text{relation disordered}}
\]

### 8.1 False independence

The core pathology is the claim of self-subsistence:

> I can preserve or enlarge myself without receiving from, answering to, or caring for the relations that sustain me.

### 8.2 Regime-level signatures of fallenness

Possible relational patterns include:

- local optimization at the cost of whole-regime viability;
- extraction without reciprocity;
- hoarding of flows needed by other members;
- domination and coerced synchronization;
- severing truthful feedback;
- excluding or silencing weak members;
- externalizing damage to distant participants or future conditions;
- treating bodies, species, land, or matter as disposable instruments;
- rigid boundaries that prevent repair;
- collapsed boundaries that erase identity and consent;
- accumulated structural drag that narrows present participation.

### 8.3 Evil as parasitic relation

Evil need not be non-relation. It may be relation organized against life: using dependence to dominate, intimacy to control, knowledge to exploit, and bonds to trap.

This prevents the false inference that stronger bonds or greater integration are automatically good.

### 8.4 The whole cannot justify sacrificing the least

Body language is dangerous when used politically or institutionally. A ruler can claim to represent “the whole” and sacrifice individuals as expendable cells.

A Christic body hypothesis must therefore satisfy a strict fruit constraint:

> **Any supposed whole-regime good that requires erasing the dignity, voice, freedom, or care of the least members is presumptively a fallen totalization, not healthy coherence.**

## 9. Redemption as Regime Healing

The working contrast is not:

\[
\text{material creation} \rightarrow \text{discarded} \rightarrow \text{spiritual escape}
\]

but:

\[
\text{creation under destructive relations}
\rightarrow
\text{healing and reconciliation}
\rightarrow
\text{embodied creation in truer relation}.
\]

### 9.1 Redemption preserves creation while transforming its pattern

A provisional formula is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{redemption}
=
\text{continuity of creation}
+
\text{transformation of relational order}
}
\]

This does not specify the degree of material continuity or the mechanics of resurrection. Those remain open.

### 9.2 Salvation

Within this module, salvation can be investigated as:

- restoration of participation;
- release from self-enclosed relation;
- capacity to receive and give love;
- reconciliation across damaged boundaries;
- freedom from death-producing patterns;
- awakening to a belonging that precedes full restoration.

Belonging may precede healing. A damaged member is still a member.

### 9.3 Faith and works

Faith need not be a credential that changes group membership, while works are added later as evidence. A relational reading is:

- **faith:** present entrusting participation in God’s life-giving relation;
- **works:** the fruit borne by that participation;
- **love:** not payment for salvation, but the relational life salvation restores.

### 9.4 Repentance

Repentance becomes a present reorientation of participation: turning from a destructive relational pattern and entering another possibility.

### 9.5 Judgment

Judgment can be researched as truthful revelation of fruit and separation of destructive pattern from beloved creation. It need not be presumed to mean that God discovers disposable pieces.

### 9.6 Kingdom now

The Kingdom becomes locally present wherever relations are reordered toward truth, mercy, service, reconciliation, shared provision, and care for the least.

This is not merely anticipation of a later world. It is the present enactment of the body’s true relational order.

### 9.7 Resurrection

Christian resurrection places a strong constraint on this framework: redemption must not solve death by declaring embodiment irrelevant.

DET’s O9-RID identity bridge remains open. This module adds a theological requirement:

> A successful resurrection account should preserve the significance of embodied relational participation while allowing transformation beyond the exact persistence of present material organization.

### 9.8 Healing, Jubilee, and κ

A reduction in structural drag may be a possible **print** of some kinds of healing. It is not identical to healing, forgiveness, grace, or Jubilee.

Any Track A κ-reduction must:

- identify the physical carrier;
- conserve energy;
- specify the channel;
- include controls;
- survive standard-variable audits.

“No physical channel” means the claim remains M/H, not that an unexplained divine term may be added.

## 10. Matter and the Non-Disposability of Creation

### 10.1 Matter is not scenery

If present existence is relational participation, matter cannot be reduced to temporary packaging around the “real” spiritual story. Bodies, air, ground, food, water, sound, tools, organisms, and ecosystems are active participants in present relational life.

### 10.2 We need matter—but matter’s value exceeds our need

Human embodiment depends on Earth and material systems. Therefore, “we do not need the Earth because it will be destroyed” contradicts present relational dependence.

However, the Earth is not valuable only because humans need it. The stronger claim is:

> Material creation has relational participation and belonging that cannot be exhausted by its usefulness to one species.

### 10.3 Patterns may end without creation being discarded

The “world” that must end may refer, at least in some scriptural contexts, to a fallen order: domination, violence, hoarding, exploitation, deceit, and death-producing organization.

This module does not pre-decide every eschatological text. It establishes a research distinction:

\[
\boxed{	ext{creation} \neq \text{the fallen pattern currently organizing creation}}
\]

### 10.4 Eschatological models to compare

| Model | Description | Key DET/Christic question |
|---|---|---|
| **Annihilation** | Material creation is erased | Why create, incarnate, heal, and resurrect embodiment if matter is finally disposable? |
| **Replacement** | This creation is discarded and another substituted | What continuity makes the renewed world genuinely the redemption of this creation? |
| **Purification** | Destructive elements or patterns are removed | Can judgment transform relation without discarding participants? |
| **Renewal/transfiguration** | Creation is deeply transformed while retaining relational continuity | Best provisional fit with embodied redemption, but must be tested textually |

### 10.5 Material continuity is not exact particle identity

A person persists through material turnover. A forest persists through birth, death, decomposition, and regrowth. Therefore, material importance does not require exact preservation of every particle.

The open question is what form of relational and embodied continuity is necessary for genuine redemption rather than replacement.

## 12. Fruit and Print Methodology

### 12.1 Definitions

**Fruit:** The present consequence a relational organization produces in participants and larger regimes.

Examples: flourishing, fear, repair, domination, resilience, exclusion, truthfulness, fragility, shared provision, or cascading harm.

**Print:** A committed, record-side trace that remains measurable after or during relational interaction.

Examples: changed bond topology, hysteresis, damage, recovery time, stress distribution, altered resource flows, memory effects, κ-like structural carrying, or persistent behavioral patterns.

**Witness:** Convergence between theological interpretation, ontological coherence, and observed fruit/prints. Witness is not deductive proof.

### 12.2 Bridge protocol

For every Track B or theological claim:

1. **State the claim and status.** Do not hide a T/M claim inside physical language.
2. **Describe expected fruit.** What would this relational ordering tend to produce?
3. **Identify possible prints.** Which ordinary measurable variables could register those effects?
4. **Specify causal channels.** Do not invoke love, grace, or consciousness as forces.
5. **Register adversaries.** Standard network science, ecology, psychology, sociology, control theory, and known physics may explain the same pattern.
6. **Pre-register failure conditions.** State what would count against the bridge hypothesis.
7. **Refuse identity inflation.** A proxy for healing is not healing itself; network integration is not love; complexity is not consciousness.
8. **Downgrade honestly.** If no distinctive signature exists, retain the claim as M/T rather than inventing one.

### 12.3 Fruit dimensions

A multi-dimensional fruit assessment is preferable to a single scalar.

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| **Viability** | Do participants and the regime remain capable of sustained life or function? |
| **Reciprocity** | Are flows one-way and extractive, or mutually sustaining? |
| **Freedom** | Does coordination preserve meaningful local agency and consent? |
| **Differentiation** | Are distinct roles and identities preserved? |
| **Care for the least** | Do vulnerable or low-power members receive protection and participation? |
| **Truthful feedback** | Can damage and need be communicated without suppression? |
| **Repair** | Can the regime acknowledge harm and restore relation? |
| **Resilience** | How does the regime respond to perturbation? |
| **Externalized harm** | Is apparent local health purchased by exporting damage elsewhere? |
| **Cross-scale integrity** | Does local benefit remain beneficial at larger and longer scales? |

No dimension alone equals love.

## 14. Track A / Computational Toy Program

### 14.1 Goal

Construct a nested-regime toy model that examines the measurable consequences of different relational organizations without inserting theological terms into equations.

### 14.2 Permitted existing DET structures

The model may use:

- nodes and event graph;
- bonds such as \(\sigma_{ij}\), \(C_{ij}\), and \(\pi_{ij}\);
- record variables;
- \(\kappa_i\) as structural history where physically defined;
- \(\Pi_i\) where its current DET definition applies;
- explicit resource, energy, damage, repair, and communication channels;
- local update rules;
- causal event ordering.

### 14.3 Forbidden shortcuts

The model must not include:

- a `love` coefficient;
- a `Christ` node controlling outcomes;
- a consciousness field;
- a grace or Jubilee force;
- a global cosmic scheduler interpreted as God;
- an agency variable;
- free κ-reduction without an energy ledger;
- a reward function silently defined as “goodness” and then presented as a discovery.

### 14.4 Nested levels

A first toy system could include four levels:

1. **Constituent nodes** — local agents or material units;
2. **Organ sub-regimes** — functionally differentiated clusters;
3. **Embodied regime** — a whole dependent on the organs;
4. **Ecological regime** — multiple embodied regimes plus material-resource cycles.

The model should be distributed and event-local, not a global simultaneous state.

### 14.5 Scenarios

#### Scenario A — Reciprocal differentiated regime

- resources circulate;
- local clusters retain autonomy;
- damage signals propagate;
- repair is targeted;
- vulnerable nodes are supported;
- no single controller dominates.

#### Scenario B — Extractive local optimization

- a sub-regime maximizes its own resource retention;
- costs are exported to weak or distant nodes;
- short-term local performance rises;
- whole-regime resilience is measured over longer horizons.

#### Scenario C — “I do not need you” removal

- low-centrality or apparently redundant nodes are removed;
- direct and delayed effects are measured;
- hidden dependencies and cascade risk are compared with naive centrality estimates.

#### Scenario D — Coercive totalization

- one controller synchronizes all nodes;
- short-term coordination may improve;
- diversity, local adaptation, failure concentration, and recovery are tested.

#### Scenario E — Restorative intervention

- damaged bonds are repaired through explicit resource and energy channels;
- burden is redistributed;
- feedback is reopened;
- recovery of local and whole-regime function is measured.

#### Scenario F — Material-disposability policy

- matter or environmental nodes are treated as unlimited waste sinks;
- delayed effects on embodied and ecological regimes are measured;
- comparison is made with closed-loop reuse and regeneration.

### 14.6 Analysis vector

A non-canonical analysis vector may be used:

\[
\mathbf{F}_G =
(V_G,
R_G,
Q_G,
D_G,
P_G,
X_G,
E_G)
\]

where, for a regime \(G\):

- \(V_G\): viability;
- \(R_G\): resilience;
- \(Q_G\): reciprocity of flows;
- \(D_G\): retained differentiation;
- \(P_G\): repair capacity;
- \(X_G\): externalized damage;
- \(E_G\): energetic/material efficiency and accounting.

This is an analysis convenience, not a physical primitive and not a quantitative definition of love.

### 14.7 Expected but non-unique results

The working expectation is that:

- extractive local optimization can produce delayed whole-regime fragility;
- support for weak nodes can preserve functions not captured by simple centrality;
- coercive synchronization can reduce adaptive diversity and create catastrophic single points of failure;
- repair with truthful feedback can improve cross-scale resilience;
- systems that externalize material damage eventually narrow their own participation possibilities;
- healthy regimes balance integration, modularity, autonomy, and reciprocity.

All of these may be explainable by ordinary systems science. That does not falsify Track B, but it means the toy model supplies **illustrative fruit**, not unique physical confirmation.

## 16. Adversarial Challenges

This proposal should be actively tested against the following objections.

### 16.1 Relation does not imply life

Correct. The module claims only relational capacity at the minimum level. Biological life, agency, and consciousness require additional organization or ontological claims.

### 16.2 Nested regimes do not entail one universal regime

Correct. A network may contain overlapping regimes without a single coherent whole. The creation-regime claim therefore requires independent argument.

### 16.3 A universal regime does not entail consciousness

Correct. Integration, complexity, and coherence are not proofs of experience.

### 16.4 Cosmic consciousness would not automatically be Christ

Correct. The Christological identification is theological, not a deduction from complexity.

### 16.5 “Body of Christ” may be church-specific

Correct. The strong creation-body reading must be established through careful textual and theological argument, not assumed from the metaphor.

### 16.6 Interdependence does not mean every present component is equally necessary

Correct. Some roles are redundant, transient, replaceable, or transformed. The claim concerns belonging and relational significance, not static indispensability.

### 16.7 Ecological or social flourishing does not prove theology

Correct. Good fruit can support comparative adequacy and expose destructive doctrine, but it cannot deductively prove God or Christ.

### 16.8 Systems can be robust and evil

Correct. Resilience and efficiency are not sufficient. A slave system can be stable. Fruit analysis must include freedom, truth, care for the least, and externalized harm.

### 16.9 Boundaries are not inherently fallen

Correct. Healthy bodies require boundaries. The question is whether boundaries regulate life-giving exchange or enforce domination, isolation, or erasure.

### 16.10 Severing a bond can be loving

Correct. Ending an abusive relation may protect the possibility of healthier relation. Love is not maximum bond persistence.

### 16.11 The body metaphor can become totalitarian

Correct. Any use of the “whole” that erases persons, suppresses dissent, or sacrifices vulnerable members fails the Christic fruit test.

### 16.12 “God is the fabric” can collapse God into creation

Correct. The preferred wording is that God is the personal source and sustaining fullness of relation, and creation participates in God through Christ. The exact metaphysical relation remains open.

1. **Relational participation may be more fundamental than the life/nonlife distinction.** This does not make every participant conscious or agentic.

12. **None of these prints uniquely proves the theology.** Their proper role is to test coherence, expose destructive consequences, guide experimental questions, and prevent empty metaphysical language.

## 21. Compact Canonical Formulations for Further Testing

### Relational Participation Principle

> Whatever presently exists does so through capacities for relation; stable objects and regimes are coherent organizations of present relational participation.

### Nested Regime Principle

> A participant can retain real identity while belonging to progressively larger regimes whose whole-level properties are not reducible to any one member.

> Healthy coherence strengthens participation while preserving difference, freedom, truthful feedback, and care for the least.

### Christic Creation Hypothesis

> All relational creation may participate in—and in the strongest formulation already constitute—the Body of Christ, whose personal unity is grounded in God’s love rather than produced by the mere sum of creation’s parts.

### Fall-as-Misrelation Hypothesis

> The Fall does not abolish relation; it disorders relation through false independence, domination, extraction, exclusion, and accumulated death-producing patterns.

### Redemptive Material Continuity Principle

> Redemption must heal and transform embodied relational creation rather than solve its brokenness by declaring matter disposable.

### Fruit/Print Rule

> DET cannot measure love, Christ, or the interior of presence directly. It may study the fruit produced by relational organizations and the prints those relations leave in the committed record, while refusing to identify any proxy with the ontological reality itself.
