## Abstract

This document describes DET's candidate continuum-limit program. The goal is to show that DET event graphs, when constructed from or compared with smooth Lorentzian manifolds, can recover causal structure, measure, metric, κ-field, and effective gravitational dynamics in the large-N limit. The current framework provides numerical evidence and partial finite theorems. Full manifoldlikeness, metric convergence, curvature convergence, and Einstein-equation emergence remain open theorem targets.

## 1. Candidate Continuum Limit Conjecture

**DET Continuum Limit Conjecture (Candidate Theorem Program).**

**Assumptions:**
1. \(M\) is a smooth globally hyperbolic \((d+1)\)-dimensional Lorentzian manifold with bounded curvature and positive injectivity radius.
2. Events are generated by a Poisson sprinkling of density \(N/V\) into a compact causal domain.
3. Each event carries record variables including Π and κ.
4. Π is bounded and positive: \(0 < \Pi_{\min} \leq \Pi_i \leq \Pi_{\max}\).
5. κ is bounded and admits a coarse-grained continuum limit: \(0 \leq \kappa_i \leq 1\).
6. Reconstruction uses causal order, Π-weighted interval information, and bond-network spatial structure.

**Conjectured large-N behavior.** With probability approaching 1 as \(N\to\infty\):

| # | Conjecture | Status |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Causal convergence: structure of G_N approximates (M, g). | Proven (causal set theory) |
| C2 | Measure convergence: appropriate empirical measure → target measure. | Numerical evidence |
| C3 | Metric convergence: reconstructed g_N → g in LGH topology. | Numerical evidence |
| C4 | κ-field convergence: coarse-grained κ_N → continuum κ. | Numerical evidence |
| C5 | Newtonian field equation emergence. | Derived + dataset consistency |
| C6 | Relativistic field equation emergence (conjectured). | Open theorem program |
| C7 | Bianchi/conservation consistency. | Combinatorial + numerical |

**Correspondence vs Emergence.** The present framework establishes a **correspondence theorem**: if a DET event graph is sampled from a Lorentzian manifold, DET reconstruction procedures recover the continuum structure in the large-N limit. It does not yet prove that DET dynamics spontaneously generate manifoldlike graphs without a background manifold. The latter is the stronger emergence program.

**Ontological principle:** The event graph is fundamental; the manifold is emergent. Proof direction: discrete → continuum. Statistical (convergence in probability). Scheduler-independent in the ensemble sense: the large-N continuum limit is invariant under admissible linear extensions of the causal partial order.

## 3. Step-by-Step Results

### Step 1: Measure Concentration
- **Modules:** `continuum_limit_step1.py`, `continuum_limit_concentration.py`
- **Bound:** \(W_1(\mu_N, \mu) \leq C_d \cdot D \cdot N^{-1/(d+1)}\) with sub-Gaussian concentration (McDiarmid, Dudley, Poisson bounds).
- **Empirical rate:** α ≈ 0.48 in 1+1 Minkowski with sinusoidal κ.
- **W₁ metric note:** The W₁ distance is computed with respect to an auxiliary complete Riemannian metric compatible with the manifold topology, or on spacelike Cauchy slices using their induced Riemannian distance.
- **Π-weighted measure clarification:** With varying Π, the limiting measure is a Π-weighted volume measure \(\mu_N^\Pi \to \Pi\,dV_g / \int \Pi\,dV_g\). Under calibration assumptions (Π asymptotically proportional to volume density), this recovers the volume measure.
- **DET advantage:** Π provides proper-time weights; bare causal sets have only event counts.

### Step 2: Metric Reconstruction
- **Module:** `continuum_limit_step2.py`
- **Empirical rate:** α ≈ 0.75 in 1+1 Minkowski with sinusoidal κ (tested regime; not established as general minimax rate).
- **Key metric:** \|\|g_N − g\|\|_F (Frobenius norm on metric components at grid points): 0.84 (N=100) → 0.08 (N=2000).
- **Reconstruction method:** Proper time from Π-weighted interval counts; metric coefficients from near-time-axis pairs; det g = −1 constraint for g_11.
- **DET advantage:** Π-weighted intervals calibrate proper time.

### Step 3: κ-Field Convergence
- **Module:** `continuum_limit_step3.py`
- **Empirical rate:** α ≈ 0.85 in 1+1 with assumed smooth continuum κ (tested regime).
- **Key metric:** L² error: 0.54 (N=100) → 0.02 (N=5000).
- **Assumptions:** Smooth continuum κ field exists; κ-dynamics (recovery+diffusion) provide regularity; coarse-graining bandwidth satisfies \(h_N \to 0, Nh_N^{d+1} \to \infty\).
- **DET advantage:** κ-dynamics (recovery+diffusion) provide regularity.

### Step 4a: Newtonian Field Equation (Implemented)
- **Modules:** `continuum_limit_step4.py`, `continuum_limit_einstein.py`, multiple dataset modules.
- **Equation:** \(\nabla^2 \Phi = 4\pi G_q \rho_\gamma\) with \(\rho_\gamma = \lambda_\gamma \kappa - \gamma_b\).
- **Evidence:** Derived in constructed models; numerically supported in continuum-limit simulations; phenomenologically consistent with 5 datasets across 12 orders of magnitude (solar system, galaxies, clusters). κ-field action converges to continuum (rel error 0.25% at N=500). Stationarity confirmed.
- **Equivalence principle:** Eötvös constraints require terrestrial materials to have nearly equal κ.

### Step 4b: Relativistic Field Equation (Conjectured)
- **Modules:** `continuum_limit_step4p.py`, `continuum_limit_geometric.py`.
- **Conjectured limit:** \(G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G_q T^\kappa_{\mu\nu}\) or a scalar-tensor-like extension.
- **Discrete action:** Benincasa-Dowker (BD) with κ-weighting. S/N O(1), stable with N. κ-weighted variant shows nonzero matter-curvature coupling.
- **Matter sector:** κ stress-energy tensor \(T^\kappa_{\mu\nu}\) derived and verified. Action convergence numerically confirmed.
- **Geometric sector:** BD action implemented for Minkowski (links/event ~2-3). de Sitter (R=const) and Schwarzschild tests needed.
- **Status:** Conjectural. Requires full BD → Einstein-Hilbert convergence.

### LGH Distance
- **Module:** `continuum_limit_lgh.py`
- **Proposed bound:** \(d_{LGH} \leq C_{causal} \cdot \varepsilon_{causal} + C_{measure} \cdot W_1\).
- **LGH definition:** Lorentzian Gromov-Hausdorff distance (Minguzzi-Suhr framework). Compares causal sets or Lorentzian metric spaces via embedding into a common metric space.
- **Key metric:** LGH bound: 3.4 (N=100) → 0.34 (N=10000) in 1+1.
- **Status:** Proposed bound; formal LGH convergence remains open.

### Bianchi Identity
- **Module:** `continuum_limit_bianchi.py`
- **Discrete Bianchi:** Removing one element changes BD action by O(1), strictly localized (link change = 1 at N=30).
- **Continuum Bianchi:** \(\nabla_\mu G^{\mu\nu} = 0\) holds identically for the Einstein tensor.
- **κ-modified conservation:** κ may exchange energy-momentum with matter:
  \[
  \nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}_m = -S^\nu_\kappa, \quad
  \nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}_\kappa = S^\nu_\kappa,
  \]
  with total conservation: \(\nabla_\mu(T^{\mu\nu}_m + T^{\mu\nu}_\kappa) = 0\). This preserves Bianchi while allowing κ nonconservation in individual sectors.

### Curvature Convergence (Implemented, Analytic Bounds Open)
- **Module:** `continuum_limit_curvature.py`
- **Estimators:** Ricci scalar (Myrheim-Meyer interval volume fit), timelike Ricci R₀₀ (proper-time distortion), sectional curvature K (causal triangle angle excess).
- **Minkowski (R=0):** Sectional curvature consistent with zero. Ricci and R₀₀ directionally correct, noisy at N<1000.
- **Status:** Three curvature estimators implemented. Sectional curvature is the most stable at tested N. Analytic convergence bounds remain open. de Sitter and Schwarzschild tests needed.

## 5. Dimensional Dependence (Baseline Expectations)

| Spacetime dimension | W₁ scaling | LGH scaling | Convergence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1+1 | \(N^{-1/2}\) | \(N^{-1/2}\) | Fastest |
| 2+1 | \(N^{-1/3}\) | \(N^{-1/3}\) | Moderate |
| 3+1 | \(N^{-1/4}\) | \(N^{-1/4}\) | Slowest |

These are baseline scaling expectations for empirical measure convergence in \(D = d+1\) spacetime dimensions. Observed metric and κ-field rates may differ because reconstruction algorithms exploit regularity, smoothness, and Π-weighting. All empirical rates above (α ≈ 0.48, 0.75, 0.85) were measured in 1+1 Minkowski with sinusoidal κ.

## 8. Proper-Time Formula (M0 Alignment)

Proper time is accumulated via event count N, not structural history κ:

\[
\Delta\tau_i = \Pi_i \cdot \Delta N_i
\]

where N is the monotone, non-decreasing event-count variable (M0 fix). κ is mutable structural history and is NOT used as the proper-time increment.
