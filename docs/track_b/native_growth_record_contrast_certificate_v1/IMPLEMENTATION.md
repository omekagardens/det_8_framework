## 3. Complete root certificate and endpoint theorem

All polynomials are dense ascending-power rational lists, with interior
zeros retained and trailing zeros removed. Zero is the empty list.
The native contrast retains its four padded coefficients; U0,U1 and
their difference retain five. The fixed division by x retains its zero
constant coefficient and all four quotient entries.

The producer computes E,j,v*,K per record and forms P from their
differences. Separately subtracting the two padded U lists must give
exactly [0]+P. This is an identity check within the fixed formulas, not
a fresh derivation of RI103 or an independent saved-certificate audit.

For nonzero degree-at-most-three P:

1. Compute P' and exact Euclidean divisions to obtain g=gcd(P,P'), made
   monic. Retain EVERY dividend, divisor, quotient and remainder.
2. Divide P by g exactly, obtaining F. Retain F, zero remainder and
   the full reconstructed product gF=P.
3. Independently apply the same Euclidean routine to F,F', retaining
   its full trace and checking monic gcd=1.
4. Build the Sturm chain F,F',-rem(F,F'),... with exact, unscaled
   negative remainders. Every quotient, raw remainder and next term
   is retained. Degrees strictly decrease. The final term is a
   nonzero constant. There is no separate monic normalization of
   individual Sturm terms: a negative rescaling could change variations.
5. Evaluate EVERY chain term exactly at 0 and R. Retain values, signs,
   the sign sequence after deleting zeros, and each variation count.
   N=V(0)-V(R) must be an integer in [0,degree(F)].

For square-free F, a root at an endpoint has nonzero derivative. At
such a root, omitting the zero leading chain value gives its right-hand
variation: on the right F has the same sign as F'. At any internal
zero of a later chain term, its neighbors have opposite signs, so
zero deletion preserves the limiting variation. Standard signed-
remainder cancellation then makes V decrease by one at each simple
root and remain constant otherwise. Thus V(0)-V(R) counts exactly the
distinct roots in (0,R], excluding zero and INCLUDING R. F and P
have the same distinct real roots.

Square-free reduction is load-bearing. For
P=(x-1)^2(x-2), R=1, the unreduced chain vanishes entirely at R:
naive zero deletion would count two instead of one. The new packet
includes this exact algebra-only regression fixture. A repeated root
counts once, which suffices for exclusion.

A generic nonzero constant has no roots and is covered by a singleton
chain. Constants are used only to test the arithmetic helper; the native
nonzero linear coefficient 6 delta-j excludes a constant native P.

No isolated intervals, root approximations or candidate rational roots
are emitted. Every zero/nonzero conclusion comes from exact rational
identities and a complete fixed-degree chain.
