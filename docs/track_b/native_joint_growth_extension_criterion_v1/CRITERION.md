# RI-38 — ratio cycles and an all-size conditional extension theorem

24 September 2026 UTC. **Proof complete; independent coordinator adjudication
accepted.** In the strictly positive, scalar-passive, fair-binary model of
[RI-34](../native_joint_growth_constraints_v1/NORMAL_FORM.md) and
[RI-36](../native_joint_growth_extension_v1/EXTENSION.md), every valid finite
prefix extends one level. An explicit inductive choice therefore gives an
all-size conditional kernel family, preserving any feedback already present
in the seed prefix.

The ratio-cycle criterion is exact, and inherited prefix consistency forces
its cycle identities. This is not inferred from finite examples: sections
3–6 give the proofs. It does **not** derive the seed, model restrictions or
extension choice from DET, make the passive payload informative, or establish
geometry, gravity or an infinite-history measure. The
[RI-38 assignment](../../../REVIEW_IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md) asks precisely
for this extension decision within the existing conditional family.

## 1. Domain and load-bearing premises

Fix `n>=1`. Assume a complete admissible prefix of rows for every finite
parent with at most `n-1` events, including all independent binary record
assignments and all its required diamonds. Keep these premises unchanged:

- A finite committed order `P`, immutable binary marks `r`, a fixed residual
  carrier and unchanged setting/type/controller/context. Every ideal is
  eligible with strictly positive probability on the full record cube.
- Exactly one committed birth per step, total ideal probability one, and
  fair newborn marks independent of the selected ideal and residual.
- Strict precursor-record locality: `q_(P,r)(S)` reads marks only in `S`.
  Unmarked order structure of `P` is permitted input, as in the predecessor
  notes. No stronger restriction to the structure of `S` alone is assumed.
- Full marked-parent equivariance, with order, ideal and record coordinates
  transported together; equal-precursor transitions remain distinct births.
- Full scalar-passive maps

  \[
  \mathcal B^{P,r}_{S,b}(D)=\frac{q_{P,r}(S)}2D,
  \qquad b\in\{0,1\},
  \tag{1}
  \]

  independent of `D`. The fixed finite carrier admits complex Hermitian PSD
  kernels of total-entry mass one, and the entire unnormalized kernel is
  retained in compositions. Scalar maps extend to the unnormalized cone.

The [original record-read permission](../../validation/t8-q-licensed-growth-2026-09-12/BIRTH_F.md)
is not weakened. Fair marks, passivity, independent full preparation cubes,
complete ideal eligibility and strict positivity are essential restrictions
of this theorem, not physical results. No silent, halt or other mass-bearing
alternative is added. Weak-zero endpoints, nonpassive instruments and varying
contexts are not covered by the construction below.

The existing prefix supplies diamonds based at parents of size at most `n-2`.
The extension supplies all rows at size `n` and every new diamond based at
size `n-1`. For `n=1` there are no earlier diamond constraints. The empty
parent has its unique ideal with probability one.

## 2. The correct finite ratio graph

### Variables and their exact quotient

Start with every size-`n` parent on a fixed labeled carrier, every **proper**
ideal `S`, and every assignment of marks to `S`. A node is the isomorphism
class

\[
 i=[P,S,r|_S].
 \tag{2}
\]

Its unknown `z_i` is the new ideal probability. The full unmarked parent
order is retained. Marks outside `S` are erased from the key because they
cannot be read. All isomorphisms must transport that whole order, the ideal
and its internal marks together. With the independent full record cube,
this is exactly the equivalence generated by record locality and complete
marked-parent equivariance; it does not identify merely isomorphic payloads
or discard the parent order.

Seed every such node, even if it has no nontrivial incident edge. The set
is finite. Distinct labeled ideals in one row can share a node, but each is
still a separate summand in normalization. The quotient is not a replacement
for the labeled transition inventory.

### Edges and full maps

For each old parent `C` of size `n-1`, each complete record assignment,
every ordered pair of ideals `S,T`, and both newborn marks `b,h`, form

\[
 P=C+x_S^b,\qquad Q=C+y_T^h,\qquad
 i=[P,T,r_P|_T],\quad j=[Q,S,r_Q|_S].
 \tag{3}
\]

The old ideals refer to the same `C`; neither contains the other newborn.
Both composed maps have the same full marked terminal after identifying
the named newborn coordinates. Their equality is

\[
 q_C(S)z_i\,D/4=q_C(T)z_j\,D/4,
 \quad\text{equivalently}\quad
 z_j=\rho_{ij}z_i,\qquad \rho_{ij}=\frac{q_C(S)}{q_C(T)}>0.
 \tag{4}
\]

Include every raw edge: loops, parallel edges, equal precursor pairs,
reversed pairs, every inherited/newborn mark and the all-zero assignment.
A loop need not be silently accepted merely because its endpoints coincide.
Stored edges are traversable backward with reciprocal gain. Thus a cycle
means a closed walk in the **bidirected completion**, not just a cycle
following arbitrary stored orientations. This matters for two distinct
paths between the same endpoints and for parallel-edge two-cycles.

Crucially, every second precursor in (3) is proper in its intermediate
parent: it excludes the first newborn. Hence **no new diamond contains a
new full-ideal probability**. Its eventual complement is unconstrained by
these new equations, though it must remain positive and normalized.

## 3. Exact extension criterion

**Theorem 1.** A positive extension of this finite prefix exists if and only
if the product of gains around every closed walk of the graph is one.

**Necessity.** Any positive solution of (4) telescopes around every closed
walk. It forces loop gains to be one, equal ratios along parallel paths,
and unit products around every longer cycle.

**Sufficiency for the ratio equations.** In each connected component choose
a root with positive potential one. Define a node's potential `p_i` by the
product of gains along a path from the root. Unit closed-walk products make
this independent of the path; each edge then satisfies (4). An isolated
node simply has potential one. A spanning forest plus every omitted edge,
loop and parallel edge is enough to check this finite condition. No branch
mass is zero, so inverse gains are defined.

**Normalization and locality.** Suppose there are `K` components. For each
component `c`, let

\[
 M_c=\max_{P,r}
 \sum_{\substack{S\subsetneq P\text{ ideal}\\ [P,S,r|_S]\in c}}
 p_{[P,S,r|_S]},\qquad
 \alpha_c=\frac1{2K(1+M_c)}.
 \tag{5}
\]

The maximum ranges over the finite complete size-`n` table. Sums count
distinct labeled ideals, including repeated appearances of the same node.
There is at least one proper slot for `n>=1`, so `K>=1`; all maxima are
finite. Set proper probabilities to `alpha_c p_i`. This preserves every
edge equation because its endpoints share a component. Each component
contributes less than `1/(2K)` to any row, so the total proper mass is less
than `1/2`. Assign the full ideal its complement, which exceeds `1/2`.

All node values and scales are fixed once from the law table, not chosen
after observing current records. Proper probabilities use only their local
node keys. The full ideal may read the complete parent records. Row sums
and their complements are invariant under marked-parent isomorphism, so
locality and equivariance hold. Full ideals are absent from new diamonds,
and earlier rows remain unchanged. This proves sufficiency and Theorem 1.

The criterion by itself does not say that an arbitrary collection of positive
edge gains is consistent. The inherited-prefix implication is proved next.

## 4. Prior path weights and an explicit local potential

For a marked order `A` of size at most `n`, define

\[
 W(A,r)=\prod_{\text{births in a natural construction of }A}
 q_{\text{preceding marked parent}}(\text{newborn's past}),
 \qquad W(\varnothing)=1.
 \tag{6}
\]

Here `W` omits the fair-bit factors: the complete scalar weight of that
particular marked history is `2^(-|A|) W(A,r)`, multiplying the full `D`.
Records are retained in the arguments of every factor. `W` is **one path
product**, not the sum of unlabeled histories or a probability normalized
over terminal marked orders.

Inherited diamonds and equivariance make `W` independent of the chosen
natural construction. Any two linear extensions of a finite poset are
connected by adjacent swaps of incomparable elements; each swap uses an
inherited diamond at a parent of size at most `n-2`, while the common later
marked state is unchanged. To see the connectivity directly, move the first
element of one extension leftward through the other: intervening elements
must be incomparable with it, then repeat on the remaining suffix.
Equivariance handles renamed copies. Strict positivity makes `W>0`.

For an available final birth `x` this gives

\[
 W(A+x_S,r+b)=W(A,r)q_{A,r}(S).
 \tag{7}
\]

The newborn's own mark is not read by this factor. A maximal event can be
placed last, so `W` is independent of each maximal event's mark, with all
other marks held fixed. This is consistent with, and does not replace,
the explicit fair-bit factors of the complete history.

### Directly local maximal-deletion formula

Take a new size-`n` parent `P` and proper ideal `S`. Define

\[
 U=\operatorname{Max}(P)\setminus S,\qquad
 u_{P,r}(S)=
 \prod_{\varnothing\ne B\subseteq U}
 q_{P\setminus B,\ r|_{P\setminus B}}(S)^{(-1)^{|B|+1}}.
 \tag{8}
\]

`U` is nonempty: an ideal containing all maxima of a finite poset contains
every vertex. Removing `B` leaves `S` intact as an ideal. Each factor is an
already defined row at a parent of size at most `n-1`; all denominators are
strictly positive. Deletion means evaluation of the fixed law on a smaller
mathematical input, **not** removal or rewriting of an actual committed record.

Every factor in (8) directly reads only `r|S`. The formula does not read
forbidden records and rely on a later cancellation to hide the read. It may
inspect the unmarked parent order to identify `U`, as the stated domain
permits. Isomorphisms carry maxima, deletion subsets, ideals and marks
together, so `u` is equivariant and descends to a positive potential on the
node quotient (2). No new state, metric or future allocated vertex is an
input to (8).

### A common terminal weight proves every new identity

For the proof only, let `H=P+y_S` be a possible terminal with a fresh
newborn `y` and either mark. Since `S` is proper,
`Max(H)=U union {y}` has at least two elements. Define the auxiliary weight

\[
 \Phi(H,r_H)=
 \prod_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(H)}
 W(H\setminus A,r_H|_{H\setminus A})^{(-1)^{|A|+1}}.
 \tag{9}
\]

All arguments of `W` have at most `n` vertices, so all are already supplied
by the inherited prefix. `H` is a mathematical comparison target, not a
preallocated future history or a construction input for the selector.

Pair the factors in (9) with deletion subsets `A=B` and `A=B union {y}`
for every nonempty `B subset U`. Their exponents are opposite. Because `y`
may be appended last with past `S`, (7) gives

\[
 W(H\setminus B)=W(P\setminus B)q_{P\setminus B}(S).
 \tag{10}
\]

The factors `W(P\B)` cancel in each pair. The remaining deletion `A={y}`
contributes `W(P)`. Thus, with the full inherited record restrictions implicit,

\[
 \Phi(H)=W(P)u_P(S).
 \tag{11}
\]

For a new diamond from old parent `C`, put
`P=C+x_S`, `Q=C+y_T`, and let `H=P+y_T=Q+x_S` after newborn transport.
The two newborns are maximal in `H`, even when `S=T`. Using (7) and (11),

\[
 q_C(S)u_P(T)=\frac{\Phi(H)}{W(C)}
            =q_C(T)u_Q(S).
 \tag{12}
\]

Both sides multiply the entire `D/4` in the two-birth maps (1). Consequently
(12) proves every new full-payload diamond, including all inherited marks,
both newborn marks, equal precursors, reversed pairs and all-zero marks.
It supplies an actual positive solution of every ratio equation, so by
Theorem 1 **every ratio cycle inherited from a valid prefix has unit product**.
No finite enumeration or numerical continuity argument is needed.

## 5. A fixed inductive selection, not just a feasibility criterion

The positive potential (8) gives an explicit choice. Define

\[
 M_n=\max_{|P|=n,\ r\in\{0,1\}^P}
          \sum_{S\subsetneq P\text{ ideal}}u_{P,r}(S),
 \qquad \varepsilon_n=\frac1{2(1+M_n)}.
 \tag{13}
\]

There are finitely many orders on a fixed `n`-vertex carrier, finitely many
mark assignments and finitely many ideals. Previously chosen rows are
strictly positive, so all factors and ratios in (8) are finite. Therefore
`0<M_n<infinity`. Isomorphic repeated representatives do not affect a maximum;
normalization still sums every distinct labeled ideal inside each row.

Set

\[
 q_{P,r}(S)=\varepsilon_n u_{P,r}(S)\quad(S\subsetneq P),\qquad
 q_{P,r}(P)=1-\varepsilon_n
                   \sum_{S\subsetneq P\text{ ideal}}u_{P,r}(S).
 \tag{14}
\]

Every proper probability is positive and their total is less than `1/2`;
the full complement exceeds `1/2`. The common level scale preserves (12).
The complement cannot enter a new diamond as a second precursor, as proved
in section 2. Thus normalization imposes no additional cycle condition.

The maximum and `epsilon_n` are chosen **once for the complete law table**,
not from the actual current record assignment. The parameter `n=|P|` is the
intrinsic size of the committed input; no external clock or observer schedule
is inserted. Equations (8), (13), (14) define one fixed recursive table rule.
At evaluation, a proper slot reads only `r|S`; the full slot is entitled to
read all parent marks. Computing the whole finite table may be expensive:
this theorem makes no efficient-implementation or physical-realizability claim.

**Theorem 2.** Every complete admissible strictly positive prefix in the
stated family extends one level by (8), (13), (14). Repeating this deterministic
construction, without changing earlier rows, defines a compatible all-size
family of conditional scalar-passive, fair-binary birth kernels. At every
finite size it satisfies normalization, strict record-read locality,
marked-parent equivariance and complete natural-label diamond covariance.

Induction is legitimate because each new level has exactly the strict
positivity and other premises needed for the next one. Equation (8) uses only
smaller-parent rows, so there is no circular definition. A finite feedback
prefix such as RI-34 or RI-36 can be the seed. Its `f(0)!=f(1)` fork-versus-
chain distinction is retained exactly; later extension does not overwrite
earlier probabilities or committed records. The theorem is not a claim that
this distinction is present in every possible seed or uniquely selected by DET.

## 6. What this resolves and what it does not

The implication left open by the assignment is resolved **within these
premises**: a valid prefix cannot encounter an inconsistent next ratio cycle.
No additional cycle-closure axiom is needed for this restricted model.
If an exact checker finds such a conflict, it must expose an inadmissible
prefix, an incorrectly formed quotient/edge, or an error in the proof or
implementation—not a permitted exception silently removed from the domain.

The normalization freedom is load-bearing. Full ideals have no independent
law specified beyond their positive complement, and all ideals are eligible.
Prescribing extra full-ideal probabilities, imposing additional structural
locality, allowing zeros, changing the record law or admitting noncommuting
nonpassive payload maps changes the problem. This note proves no extension
theorem for those alternatives. All-zero **records** are included; zero
**probability** endpoints remain excluded.

The result is an all-size conditional mathematical family, not a derivation
of that family from the DET axioms. The seed, fair records, passive residual,
domain and selection (13)–(14) are extra chosen structure. No metric,
coordinates, target mesh, borrowed growth kernel, physical Hilbert space or
amplitudes are inserted, but passing that input test does not establish a
native quantum-derived birth map. The residual remains passive.

An infinite-history measure and its covariant event algebra, typical width
or order regime, local measure, manifold structure, clock/readout dynamics,
matter/source response and gravity are distinct obligations not constructed
or analyzed here. In particular, a labeled path-measure construction can use
normalized kernels once its initial-state and measurable-space setup is made;
this note neither carries out that construction nor identifies its covariant
event algebra or physical interpretation. No informative quantum coupling, unique
physical law, empirical discriminator or ontology validation is claimed.
Option B, metric-as-record Status M, the RET pause and protected-validation
prerequisites are unchanged.

corroboration tool, capped at **new parent size four**: prior rows have sizes
zero through three, new diamonds have three-event bases and five-event
terminals. It does not implement or enumerate an unbounded family.

The prefix formulas and small enumeration helpers are re-expressed from the
accepted RI-36 note/checker; neither is imported or changed. Four rational

revalidates all 67 prefix rows, 353 ideal slots, 1,199 locality comparisons,

This reuse is explicit, not claimed as an independently invented prefix.

Structural enumeration closes every subset of forward relations and
deduplicates complete poset relations. For the new layer it retains 40
naturally labeled parents. The quotient key includes the **whole** parent
order, proper ideal and only internal marks, minimized over all permutations.
All nodes are seeded before edges; duplicate raw edges and labeled row
summands are retained. The graph solver adds reciprocal traversals and
checks consistency of exact rational potentials. A reported conflict carries
a verified closed raw-edge walk and its nonunit gain product.

Independently of that graph traversal, the checker evaluates the deletion
product (8), explicitly transporting the induced relation, retained record
coordinates and precursor after each deletion. It checks that this potential
has a single value per quotient node and satisfies every raw edge. It then
computes the maximum labeled proper-row sum over the complete fixed layer,
chooses (13) once, and validates all normalized rows (14). This is a full-table
corroborator, not an apparatus or mechanically access-controlled birth selector:
its locality checks test equality of values under changes outside the ideal.
The proof of direct local dependence is the factorwise statement in section 4.
