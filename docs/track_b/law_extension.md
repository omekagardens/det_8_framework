# DET v8.0 — Track B: F10 Compression Hypothesis & Law Extension

**Date:** August 10, 2026
**Status:** Major F10 refinement
**Question:** If past law is immutable, can the present extend law — adding new laws without degrading old ones?

---

## 1. The Core Question Reframed

Traditional F10 framing: "Is L fixed or does it evolve?" This is a binary question that forces either eternalism (L never changes) or relativism (L can be anything).

The user's reframing: **Can L GROW?** Not change — extend. Add new branches without breaking old ones. Like a constitution that can be amended but whose original articles remain in force.

This is a third option the binary framing missed:

| Position | L status | New facts? | New laws? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Eternalism** | Fixed forever | Yes (within L) | No |
| **Relativism** | Can change arbitrarily | Yes | Yes (replaces old) |
| **DET Extensionism** | Immutable base + growing branches | Yes (within L) | **Yes (extends L)** |

## 2. How Law Extension Works

### 2.1 The compression hypothesis

F10 proposed: L is the stable compression of all prior records. As more records accumulate, the compression may need EXTENSION — new patterns that weren't visible in the smaller dataset.

A compression that worked for N records may need refinement for 2N records. The old compression is not WRONG — it was the best fit for the data available. The new compression is BETTER — it captures patterns the old one missed.

### 2.2 DET formalization

Let L_t be the law map at time t, generated from records up to t:

\[
L_t = \text{compress}(\mathcal R_{\leq t})
\]

At time t+1, new records arrive. If the old compression L_t still fits the expanded dataset, no extension is needed. If it doesn't, L is extended:

\[
L_{t+1} = \text{extend}(L_t, \mathcal R_{t+1})
\]

subject to the constraint:

\[
\boxed{L_{t+1}(\mathcal R^-) \supseteq L_t(\mathcal R^-) \quad \forall \mathcal R^-}
\]

**The extension constraint:** The new law map must generate AT LEAST the possibilities the old one did. Nothing that was lawful becomes unlawful. The possibility space only GROWS.

### 2.3 What this means for κ

κ is the present structural imprint of past events. If L evolves through compression of records, then κ is the WEIGHT of those records in determining L. High-κ systems (heavily constrained by history) would resist L-extension because the old records dominate the compression. Low-κ systems (structurally free) would more readily admit new patterns.

## 3. Observable Consequences

### 3.1 No "law violation" is possible

Since L_{t+1} ⊇ L_t, anything that was lawful remains lawful. What APPEARS to be a "violation" of old law is actually the discovery of a new branch that was always possible but not yet encoded in L.

### 3.2 The arrow of law growth

L can only GROW — new branches added, old branches retained. This is a monotonicity property analogous to record growth. Just as the record R only grows (new facts added, none removed), the law map L only extends (new possibilities added, none removed).

### 3.3 κ resists law extension

High-κ systems constrain Ω — they have fewer possibilities. This means they also resist L-extension because the old records (which carry high κ) dominate the compression. To extend L, κ must be low — structural freedom is needed for law discovery, just as it is for faith.

### 3.4 Grace enables law discovery

Grace (Ω-enlargement) doesn't just open new possibilities within existing law. It opens the possibility of law EXTENSION — of discovering that the law was bigger than we thought.

## 4. The Compression Hypothesis — Formalized

**Conjecture:** L is the minimal program that generates Ω from R⁻ for all observed records. As more records accumulate, the minimal program may need to grow, but never contradict its earlier outputs.

**Testable:** Can we construct a sequence of records where the minimal generating program necessarily grows? If yes, law extension is forced by the data.

**Connection to computational irreducibility:** If the sequence of records is computationally irreducible, then no finite compression can capture it. L must grow without bound. This means law discovery is an open-ended process — the universe's laws are not a closed book but an ever-expanding library.

## 5. The Deepest Formulation

> The past cannot be changed. But the law that describes the past can be discovered to be larger than we thought. New laws are not violations of old laws — they are the discovery that the old laws were special cases of something more general. The universe's lawfulness is not a closed book but a growing tree: roots fixed, branches still growing.
