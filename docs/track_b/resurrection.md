## 1. Why Resurrection Is the Central Track-B Test

Resurrection is not merely another phenomenon. It forces all four Track-B deadlocks into one claim:

> A person truly dies; the death remains an irreversible fact; later, that same person — not merely a copy, memory, simulation, or social influence — is again bodily and presently alive.

| DET8 area | Resurrection's test |
|---|---|
| **Record-growth time** | Can death remain committed while a new living future becomes possible? |
| **Mutable carrying (κ)** | Can death lose its final structural dominance without being erased? |
| **Persistent identity** | What makes the restored person numerically the same person rather than an exact reconstruction? |
| **Agency** | What distinguishes living presence from a perfect behavioral replay? |
| **Embodiment** | Is resurrection renewed bodily participation or merely preserved information? |
| **Relativity and locality** | Can resurrection be a local commit event without requiring a universal cosmic "now"? |
| **Physical governance** | Can divine action be confessed without inserting an unexplained force into Track A? |

## 2. The Duplicate Test — PID-C/PID-M Split

The decisive adversary: imagine two physically distinct reconstructions containing exactly the same memories, personality, bodily pattern, and relational history. If record equality alone makes each reconstruction the original person, both are numerically identical to the original — and therefore to each other. But they occupy different places and immediately undergo different events.

\[
\boxed{\text{Exact record or pattern equality is not sufficient for numerical personal identity.}}
\]

### PID-C — Persistent Identity, Construction (analogous to NPF-C)
A code-auditable, unique causal-relational lineage links the earlier and later embodied regimes, with no duplicate claimant or hidden identity token. Modelable as record-structural continuity.

### PID-M — Persistent Identity, Metaphysical (analogous to NPF-M)
The later regime is numerically the same living self. Not automatically proven by PID-C. Preserves DET8's agency and consciousness quarantine — no `soul_id`, `identity_field`, or divine selector is introduced.

## 3. What DET8 Already Handles Well

**Death is not undone:**

\[
d \prec r
\]

The death event \(d\) remains in the causal past of the resurrection event \(r\). The later record extends rather than replaces:

\[
\mathcal R_r \supset \mathcal R_d
\]

Wounds and history can remain legible while their present carrying (κ) changes. Death remains true without remaining final.

**DET8 already prevents these shortcuts:**
- Resurrection ≠ κ → 0. Structural recovery is not personal restoration.
- A simulation of someone's records is not automatically that person.
- Grace cannot be invoked as a physical state-changing function without a causal channel.
- Any bodily reconstitution must satisfy locality, conservation, and falsification.

## 6. Proposed New Open Problem: O9-RID

**O9-RID — Resurrection Identity Bridge**

> What establishes numerical personal identity across the complete interruption of ordinary embodied continuity, without erasing death, reducing the person to stored information, or inserting a hidden identity token into the physics?

**Status:** Open. PID-C provides necessary conditions (unique causal lineage). PID-M addresses the metaphysical question.

**Promotion criteria:** A DET8-consistent account of the identity bridge that satisfies:
1. Death remains committed and irreversible as an event.
2. No retroactive editing of the death record.
3. The resurrected person is numerically identical, not merely similar.
4. No `soul_id`, `identity_field`, or divine selector enters Track A.
5. The account is compatible with local commit events (no global "now").
6. Agency and consciousness remain Status M (quarantined).

## 7. Decision Gates

- **No retroactive deletion:** Death event d cannot be removed from R⁻.
