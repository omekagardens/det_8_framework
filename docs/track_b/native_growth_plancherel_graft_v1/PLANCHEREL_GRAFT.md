## 2. Intrinsic weights with all diagram multiplicities

For a partition \(\lambda=(\lambda_1\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_\ell>0)\), let
\(P_\lambda\) be its cell order:
\((i,j)\leq(i',j')\) iff both coordinates are no larger.
An oriented diagram means this specified row/column convention: conjugate
partitions are separate diagrams unless they are equal. Cells/coordinates
are auxiliary to the definition below, not additional runtime inputs.

A finite labeled order F is Ferrers when it is isomorphic to at least one
such diagram. Define

\[
 {\rm Emb}(F)=\coprod_{\lambda:P_\lambda\cong F}
                 {\rm Iso}(F,P_\lambda),\quad
 b(F)=|{\rm Emb}(F)|,\quad
 e(F)=\#\{\hbox{linear extensions of }F\},
\]
\[
 w(F)=\frac{b(F)e(F)}{|F|!},\qquad w(\varnothing)=1.
 \tag{1}
\]

Set \(w(Q)=0\) for non-Ferrers Q. All sums are finite. The empty partition
contributes its one empty isomorphism, with
\(b(\varnothing)=e(\varnothing)=1\). We do **not** assume
that conjugation accounts for every possible isomorphism between diagram
orders. Each admissible diagram and every isomorphism contribute. If there
are \(m(F)\) admissible diagrams, then \(b(F)=m(F)|{\rm Aut}(F)|\);
this is a counting identity, not an extra factor to insert in transition
probabilities. In particular, self-conjugacy and internal automorphisms
are not interchangeable.

For each **individual labeled ideal** S of a Ferrers parent F, adjoin one
new maximal vertex with strict past S, denoting the child by \(F+x_S\).
Define

\[
 q_F(S)=\frac{w(F+x_S)}{w(F)}.
 \tag{2}
\]

The denominator is positive on this parent domain. Non-Ferrers children
have zero weight. The rule is independent of all marks. Section 4 gives
the convention for zero-support histories; (2) alone does not define a
normalized law on every non-Ferrers parent.

## 3. Normalization, without merging ideals

### Young branching identity

On the formal finite-support vector space generated by diagrams, let
\(\mathsf U\) add one cell and \(\mathsf D\) remove one cell,
summing over all allowed corners.
These are formal incidence operators, not a Hilbert-space assumption.
For distinct same-size diagrams, a common upper cover exists precisely
when a common lower cover does; they are the union and intersection,
respectively, and each is unique. Thus off-diagonal coefficients of
\(\mathsf D\mathsf U\) and \(\mathsf U\mathsf D\) agree.
On the diagonal the number of addable corners exceeds the
number of removable corners by one, including at the empty diagram.
Therefore \(\mathsf D\mathsf U-\mathsf U\mathsf D=I\).

The coefficient of \(\lambda\) in \(\mathsf U^n\varnothing\) counts saturated Young
paths, equivalently standard tableaux or linear extensions of \(P_\lambda\).
Using \(\mathsf D\varnothing=0\) gives
\(\mathsf D\mathsf U^{n+1}\varnothing=(n+1)\mathsf U^n\varnothing\), hence

\[
 \sum_{\Lambda:\Lambda\text{ covers }\lambda} e(P_\Lambda)
      =(n+1)e(P_\lambda).
 \tag{3}
\]

This is a self-contained proof of the standard branching identity used here.

### Embedding/corner bijection

There is a bijection between

\[
 \{(\phi,a):\phi\in{\rm Emb}(F),\
                a\text{ addable to its diagram}\}
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 \coprod_{S\text{ ideal of }F}{\rm Emb}(F+x_S).
 \tag{4}
\]

Forward: pull back the old cells below a through \(\phi\), obtaining one
labeled ideal S, and extend \(\phi\) by mapping the newborn to a.
Backward: the newborn is maximal in the child diagram, hence a removable
corner; delete that corner and restrict the embedding. Its remaining
diagram is a Young diagram because a finite order ideal of a Young cell
order is again a Young diagram.

Weight each pair in (4) by the extension count of its child. Equation (3)
then yields

\[
 \sum_{S\text{ ideal}}b(F+x_S)e(F+x_S)
       =(n+1)b(F)e(F),\qquad
 \sum_S q_F(S)=1.
 \tag{5}
\]

Multiple corner/embedding choices producing the same S are already counted
in \(b(F+x_S)\). Distinct ideals remain separate summands, including ideals
related by a parent automorphism. No orbit-size or additional corner factor
multiplies (2).

### Hand controls

Weights are \(w(1)=w(C_2)=1\), \(w(C_3)=1/3\), \(w(V_3)=2/3\),
\(w(\square_4)=1/6\), and \(w(H_4)=1/4\) for each labeled unequal-arm hook.
Consequently:

| Parent | Individual precursor | Probability |
|---|---|---:|
| Singleton | Full | 1 |
| Two-chain | Full | 1/3 |
| Two-chain | Its singleton minimum, producing a fork | 2/3 |
| Three-fork | Full, producing the four-cell square | 1/4 |
| Three-fork | Minimum plus first arm | 3/8 |
| Three-fork | Minimum plus second arm | 3/8 |

All other ideals in these rows have zero probability. In particular the two

## 4. Equivariance, locality and full zero-aware diamonds

The weights depend only on order isomorphism classes. Transporting a parent,
ideal and marking therefore transports the probability unchanged. Since (2)
reads no marks, it satisfies the stated precursor-record locality.

For a Ferrers base C and any ordered pair of its ideals S,T, the two
newborns are incomparable, with common terminal
\(Q=C+x_S+y_T\). This includes S=T, full precursors, reversals and loops.

If Q is Ferrers, both intermediate orders are its maximal-deletion ideals,
hence Ferrers. Both unmultiplied path products are exactly

\[
 q_C(S)q_{C+x_S}(T)
   =\frac{w(Q)}{w(C)}
   =q_C(T)q_{C+y_T}(S).
 \tag{6}
\]

If Q is not Ferrers, each route has a zero edge: either its intermediate
is already unsupported, or its second child weight is zero. A zero first
edge makes that full two-step history have mass zero; we never divide by
the unsupported intermediate's weight. This is a statement about the
Ferrers-supported history process, not a claim that (2) supplies arbitrary
off-support rows. The global graft below supplies those rows explicitly.

For either newborn-bit pair, the complete scalar maps on the unchanged
unnormalized carrier agree: each is its path product times D/4. Zeros give
the zero map. There is no orbit multiplier, conditioning on a positive
branch, trace renormalization or deletion of an inconvenient zero case.

## 5. Exact Plancherel pushforward, including the Markov kernel

A Young path from the empty diagram to \(\lambda\) has product probability
\(f^\lambda/n!\) under the standard growth ratios; \(f^\lambda=e(P_\lambda)\).
Label each added cell by its birth index and forget its cell coordinates.
The result is a naturally labeled Ferrers order F.

Conversely, each \(\phi\in{\rm Emb}(F)\) specifies exactly one such hidden
Young path: images of initial birth-label sets are ideals/Young subdiagrams.
Every compatible hidden path has probability \(e(F)/n!\).
Thus the probability of the **particular naturally labeled F** is

\[
 \Pr(F)=\frac{b(F)e(F)}{n!}=w(F).
 \tag{7}
\]

A natural order includes all its earlier birth restrictions, so this also
describes its whole projected history. Given that history, the compatible
embeddings are uniform. The conditional embedding/corner calculation (4)
therefore gives exactly (2); this is a Markov pushforward, not just matching
one-time marginals. Independent fair records multiply each hidden history
by the same \(2^{-n}\) and do not change that conditional calculation.

For an abstract unlabeled order class [F], the number of distinct natural
order realizations is \(e(F)/|{\rm Aut}(F)|\). Automorphisms act freely on
linear extensions, so

\[
 \Pr([F])=\frac{e(F)}{|{\rm Aut}(F)|}w(F)
     =\sum_{\lambda:P_\lambda\cong F}\frac{(f^\lambda)^2}{n!}.
 \tag{8}
\]

This is the precise diagram-Plancherel pushforward over **all** matching
oriented shapes. It does not identify an abstract order with one preferred
diagram or assert a classification of diagram-order ambiguities.

\[

 q^{G}_{P,r}(T)=0\text{ on every nonlifted ideal}.
 \tag{10}

### Unique core and hereditary recognition

In (9), any ideal meeting F contains all k vertices of K plus at least one
suffix vertex. It therefore has size at least k+1. An ideal of size k
must be exactly K. This proves uniqueness and intrinsic equivariance;
uniqueness is not a chronological labeling hypothesis.

Every maximum lies in F. If \(|P|>k+1\), deleting any maximal vertex leaves
the same K and a nonempty Ferrers ideal \(F-v\). Thus maximal deletion
preserves the family above that boundary. At size k+1 it leaves just the
core, which is handled by the size-k full-only rule.

In particular, an off-family parent of size greater than k cannot have
**any** family one-birth child: deleting that child's newborn maximum
would contradict the preceding fact. This property, not a convenient
choice of fallback on reachable paths, makes the global zero cases safe.
Ferrers orders are not hereditary under arbitrary vertex deletion; only
the stated ideal/maximal-deletion fact is being used.

Fix any marked base C, every ordered pair of ideals S,T, and both newborn

**Base size k.** Both first rows are full-only. If neither first precursor

first weight is already zero. If both precursors are full, the common

**Base size greater than k, inside the family.** Write C=K⊕F.

each route has a zero first or second edge. A nonfamily intermediate
uses full-only fallback, but the remaining precursor still omits its
first newborn and is proper, so its fallback weight is zero too.

**Base size greater than k, outside the family.** Every child remains
outside by hereditary recognition. Its full-only fallback makes every
second incomparable-birth edge zero, including when the first edge was
full and nonzero. Both products are zero.

These cases prove every unmultiplied scalar diamond, not merely the
