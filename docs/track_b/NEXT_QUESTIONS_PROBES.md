1. Why assume that death and decay are eternally necessary mechanisms of life rather than current mechanisms used by an incompletely coordinated creation?

From a necrocycle to living circulation
Present ecological life frequently follows a pattern like:
\[
G_i
\xrightarrow{\text{death}}
\mathcal M_i
\xrightarrow{\text{decomposition}}
\mathcal M_{\text{available}}
\xrightarrow{\text{uptake}}
G_j.
\]The first regime dies, its organized material is broken down, and another regime uses what becomes available.
My proposed kingdom pattern would be closer to:
\[
G_i
\xrightarrow{\text{living gift or reconfiguration}}
\left(G_i',\,\Delta\mathcal M_{i\rightarrow j}\right)
\xrightarrow{\text{relational transfer}}
G_j',
\]where \(G_i\) remains a living participant rather than becoming the raw material required for \(G_j\).
This could be called living circulation:
Resources move through gift, overflow, secretion, exchange, mutual provision, transformation, and shared production rather than requiring the terminal destruction of the provider.

A tree does not fundamentally require another organism’s corpse as such. It requires energy, water, carbon, minerals, and relational partners capable of making resources available. Present forests often depend heavily on decomposition to make nutrients accessible, but decomposition is a current supply channel—not a logical proof that death is the only possible channel.
A different creational organization would have to provide every needed element through living transfer or direct provision.
This gives us a sharper distinction:
\[
\boxed{
\text{life sustained by death}
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
\text{life sustained by living gift}.
}
\]Current creation contains partial hints of this: sunlight nourishing plants, organisms producing food without dying, symbiotic exchange, maternal provision, shared chemical signaling, and mutual support. None is a complete deathless ecology, but they show that nourishment does not always require destruction of the provider.

That would mean:
forms may change;
resources may move;
bonds may be reconfigured;
bodies may be transformed;
gifts may be given;
new regimes may emerge;
but no participant is abandoned from the fabric of living relation.
The strongest formulation is not a static universe in which everything remains exactly as it is. It is:
Transformation without disposability; circulation without terminal exclusion; self-giving without the final annihilation of the giver.

TB-RC2 — Living Circulation, Nested Agency, and the Contingency of Death
Its core hypotheses could be:
Agency Non-Exclusion Principle
Failure to demonstrate agency at a lower scale does not establish non-agency. Organs, cells, neural systems, and underlying relational structures remain ontologically open cases.

Ministerial Whole Principle
Higher-regime integration is rightly used to support the communication, provision, repair, and meaningful participation of lower regimes, not to erase their differentiation.

Endogenous Provision Principle
A regime should explore whether distributed members can provide resources, signals, or regulatory support needed by an ailing member.

Open-System Gift Principle
Receiving medicine, nourishment, knowledge, or care from outside the body is not a failure of internal agency; it may be provision from the larger regime in which the body participates.

Death-Contingency Hypothesis
The present biological and ecological usefulness of death does not establish its ontological necessity. Death may be a provisional solution used by regimes with limited repair, communication, resources, and coordination.

Living Circulation Hypothesis
The functions currently performed through death and decomposition may, under a more deeply integrated relational order, be performed through living transfer, gift, repair, reconfiguration, and transformed embodiment.

Zero-Perishing Principle
Redemption need not preserve every current configuration unchanged, but it should not solve creation’s difficulties by treating genuine participants as irretrievably disposable.
