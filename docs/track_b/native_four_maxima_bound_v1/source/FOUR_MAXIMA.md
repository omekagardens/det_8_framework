## 2. Complete marked domain and omission identity

Let C={1,2,3,4} be the maxima of the six-parent P and R=P minus C.
R is a two-event chain or antichain. Each cap i has a precursor ideal
E_i in R, and

    E_1 union E_2 union E_3 union E_4 = R.                        (5)

Every vertex lies below some maximum, proving coverage. Conversely
these data give exactly four maxima. All compatible repetitions and
empty/full precursors, all natural labelings and all old/cap marks are
included. Distinct labeled ideal occurrences are never identified merely
because they share a quotient key.

For each proper J subset C let q_J be the actual complete row on
Q_J=R plus the caps in J above their ORIGINAL E_i, with original
restricted marks. Write B=q_empty. Concatenated indices denote subsets,
not multiplication. The general proper-slot definition is

    u6(P,S)=product_(nonempty H subset Max(P) minus S)
                     q_(P minus H)(S)^((-1)^(|H|+1)).            (6)

No old maximum outside C exists in P. Extra old maxima can reappear in
its deleted parents, and will be retained inside their potentials.

If S omits only cap i, (6) has one factor q_(C minus i)(S).
Let L_i be the mass in this complete five-parent row of ideals not
containing every other cap. Empty is excluded from the single-omission
sector, whereas the full five-parent is included. Positivity gives
0<L_i<1. The four single-omission sectors sum to 4-L, with 0<L<4.

If exactly two caps j,k are omitted, the retained pair is I=C minus
{j,k}. Write S=A union I, where A is an ideal of R containing every
E_i for i in I. There are six such sectors:

    C2_I = sum_A q_(I+j)(S)q_(I+k)(S)/q_I(S).                    (7)

The two single deletions give the two five-parent numerators; deletion
of both omitted caps gives the four-parent denominator.

If exactly three caps j,k,l are omitted, only i is retained and
S=A+i with E_i subset A. All seven deletion factors give

    C3_i = sum_A q_i(S) q_ijk(S)q_ijl(S)q_ikl(S)
                          /[q_ij(S)q_ik(S)q_il(S)].             (8)

Finally, if all four caps are omitted, S=A ranges over EVERY ideal of R.
The fifteen factors, grouped by deletion size, are

    C4 = sum_(A in J(R))
          [product_(|J|=3) q_J(A)] [product_(|J|=1) q_J(A)]
          /[B(A) product_(|J|=2) q_J(A)].                       (9)

There are four size-five numerator factors from single deletions, six
size-four denominators from double deletions, four size-three numerators
from triple deletions, and the size-two denominator from deletion of
all four caps. Thus independently of any later estimate,

    U6 = 4-L + sum_(|I|=2) C2_I + sum_i C3_i + C4.              (10)

Every proper ideal appears once. A=R occurs in (7)-(9), including the
FULL four-parent denominator q_I(R+I) in (7). In contrast, all the
four-parent entries in (8) are proper, even when A=R.

## 5. Retained cap lemma for the four triple sectors

For (8), set S=A+i in the three-parent Q_i=R+i. We prove

    q_ij(S)q_ik(S)q_il(S) >= q_i(S)epsilon^2/16.                 (13)

The omitted precursors E_j,E_k,E_l exclude i, so they do NOT cover
Q_i. The earlier covering-precursor lemma cannot be transplanted without
this separate proof. All entries on the left of (13) are proper slots.

First suppose A is nonempty. At most one vertex of R lies outside A.
If one exists, E_i subset A excludes it from E_i, so original coverage
puts it in at least one omitted precursor E_h. In Q_(i+h), all old
maxima outside S are then covered by h. If A=R there are no such old
vertices and any h works. In either case h is the sole omitted maximum
of Q_(i+h), and

    u4(Q_(i+h),S)=q_i(S).

Its proper-slot bound gives q_(i+h)(S)>=q_i(S)/8. The other two
four-parent factors are at least epsilon, proving the stronger
q_i(S)epsilon^2/8.

It remains that A is empty, so E_i is empty and S={i}. If R is a
chain, coverage of its top requires some omitted E_h=R. Its extension
again leaves only h omitted; selected i remains a maximum in S.
The preceding stronger bound applies.

If R is the two-antichain and some omitted E_h=R, the same argument
works. Otherwise the three omitted precursors are proper subsets of R.
To cover its two vertices, two distinct indices h,t must have the two
distinct singleton precursors. The base Q_i is the three-antichain.
Using e=1/44, d=1/4 and g=1/10, the literal seed gives

    q_i({i})=eg/d=(2/5)e,
    u4(Q_(i+h),{i})=u4(Q_(i+t),{i})=q_i({i})e/g=4e^2.          (14)

For each potential in (14), its two omitted maxima are the new cap
and the other old vertex of R. Deleting them singly gives the selected
singleton probability q_i({i}) in the three-antichain and the selected
isolate probability e in an edge-plus-isolate. Deleting both gives the
two-antichain singleton probability g. All three factors are present.

Their product divided by q_i({i}) is 40e^3, which exceeds
4epsilon=(9/2)e^3 since epsilon=9e^3/8. The two proper-slot scale
bounds then give

    q_(i+h)(S)q_(i+t)(S) >= q_i(S)epsilon/16.

The third factor is at least epsilon, proving (13). This exhausts the
two possible base shapes and all A. Every actual record remains; the
entries in (14) are record-independent by the fixed seed, not a chosen
zero-record sample.

Substitution into (8) and complete strict subrow sums give
