# Half-scale event availability for one fixed eligible prefix

21 October 2026. Conditional manual finite-prefix theorem.
No implementation, native numerical evaluation or experiment.
Independent review is recorded separately from this source manuscript.

## 1. Answer and scope

**Yes, conditionally on one actual eligible prefix and its own regular
construction.** The already admitted optional full-layer half-scale
prescription supplies a strictly positive symbolic lower bound for
diagnostic-event availability under both baseline and perturbed laws,
uniformly on that prefix's fixed compact positive-lambda neighborhood
and over all old markings. It also survives the within-label mixtures
specified below.

Write
\[
 c_4=\frac1{2(1+63m_4^{-31})},\qquad
 \lambda_-=\min\mathcal I>0,\qquad
 e_4=\frac1{352},\quad c_1=\frac{3231}{44000}.
\]
Then a common bound is
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \nu_*:=c_4\lambda_-^2\theta^2(e_4+c_1)>0,\qquad
 p_B(K_0\text{ or }K_4),\ p_Q(K_0\text{ or }K_4)\ge\nu_* .
 \quad}                                                       \tag{1}
\]
Here \(m_4,\theta,\mathcal I\) belong to the **same fixed prefix and
its own regular neighborhood**, not independently selected scalar inputs.

Under the additional exact-category iid access assumptions, the accepted
diagnostic-label error result and (1) yield a finite **prospective**
raw cap for any declared abstention target \(0<\zeta<1\):
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 N_\zeta(m)=
 \left\lceil\max\left\{
 m,\ \frac{2(m-1)}{\nu_*},\
 \frac{2\log(1/\zeta)}{\nu_*^2}
 \right\}\right\rceil .
 \quad}                                                       \tag{2}
\]
This cap gives coverage at least \(1-\zeta\). With the previously
certified conditional type-II tolerance \(\alpha_{\rm II}\), power
is at least \((1-\alpha_{\rm II})(1-\zeta)\).

This is restricted finite-observation feasibility, not a uniform result
over arbitrary positive scales, all prefixes, noisy readouts or native
growing trajectories. No actual \(\Xi>1\) occurrence, numerical event
rate, numerical cap or physical access is established here.
The existing \(m=40000,N=400000,k=38825\) design is unchanged.
Equation (2) defines a distinct hypothetical design/theorem, not a
retuning or permission to execute it.

## 3. Entire marked-domain half-scale and its lower bound

For every marked parent of size five, keep the admitted family
\[
 q_\lambda(P,r,S)=
 \begin{cases}
  \lambda q^0(P,r,S),&S\subsetneq P,\\
  1-\lambda+\lambda q^0(P,r,P),&S=P ,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad 0<\lambda\le1.                                       \tag{7}
\]
Every parent of size at most four is unchanged. Full entries in (7)
are complete normalization complements, not complements of selected
diagnostic support.

For a six-parent proper ideal let
\(E(P,S)=\operatorname{Max}(P)\setminus S\ne\varnothing\), and use
the accepted deletion potential
\[
 u_\lambda(P,r,S)=
 \prod_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq E(P,S)}
 q_\lambda(P\setminus A,r|_{P\setminus A},S)^{(-1)^{|A|+1}}.
                                                               \tag{8}
\]
All induced markings and smaller full slots are retained.
Set
\[
 T_{P,r}(\lambda)=\sum_{S\subsetneq P,\ S\ {\rm ideal}}
                       u_\lambda(P,r,S),\qquad
 M_6(\lambda)=\max_{|P|=6,\ r:P\to\{0,1\}}T_{P,r}(\lambda).
\]
The sum counts every individual proper ideal, including the empty one.
The maximum is over the **whole marked six-parent domain**.
A finite labeled representative domain with all markings suffices by
equivariance; no support-only or root-only maximum is substituted.

The selected optional half-scale law is
\[
 \rho^H_\lambda=\frac1{2(1+M_6(\lambda))},\qquad
 q^H_\lambda(P,r,S)=\rho^H_\lambda u_\lambda(P,r,S)
       \quad(S\subsetneq P),
\]
\[
              q^H_\lambda(P,r,P)=1-\rho^H_\lambda T_{P,r}(\lambda).
                                                               \tag{9}
\]
Its complete compatible construction is an admitted premise of the
baseline-scale source, not a new proof of existence from bare DET.
In particular,
\[
 q^H_\lambda(P,r,P)\ge\tfrac12+\rho^H_\lambda>\tfrac12.
                                                               \tag{10}
\]

For completeness, the accepted coarse bound can be checked directly.
Define the full fixed-prefix minimum
\[
 m_4=\min_{\substack{|Q|\le4,\ r:Q\to\{0,1\}\\
                     S\ {\rm any\ ideal\ of}\ Q}}
                        q^0(Q,r,S)>0.                         \tag{11}
\]
This includes full ideals, all old marks and the empty parent.
Finite representative domains and complete positivity give
\(0<m_4\le1\).

Every negative exponent in (8) corresponds to a nonempty even subset
\(A\), so \(|A|\ge2\). Its denominator belongs to a fixed parent of
size at most four and is at least \(m_4\).
If \(k=|E(P,S)|\le6\), there are \(2^{k-1}-1\le31\) such denominator
occurrences, counted with multiplicity. Numerator probabilities are
at most one, and there are at most \(2^6-1=63\) individual proper
ideals in a six-parent row. Consequently
\[
 u_\lambda(P,r,S)\le m_4^{-31},\qquad
 M_6(\lambda)\le63m_4^{-31},\qquad
 \rho^H_\lambda\ge c_4:=\frac1{2(1+63m_4^{-31})}>0.             \tag{12}
\]
This bounds the original whole-domain maximum; it does not replace it.

The factors in (8) are positive continuous functions of \(\lambda\);
their finite sums and finite maximum are continuous. Thus the selected
scale and full baseline rows are continuous. No maximizing-row
selection, coefficient allocation or numerical interval is needed.
The stronger global scale bound (12) holds for \(0<\lambda\le1\), but
does not enlarge the perturbation neighborhood or admit \(\lambda=0\).

The regular harmonic equations have the same positive six-parent
scale as a cancellable common factor. The accepted regular section
and the **same** amplitude (5) therefore apply to (9).
Multiply each supported proper birth probability by its admitted
\(h_j(\lambda)=1+\eta_*v_j(\lambda)\), with multiplier one off that
support; the full birth is unchanged. Complete marked harmonicity
makes the total probability change zero in every affected row.
All slots, not only \(K_0,K_4\), remain, and multiplier positivity
gives complete positive perturbed rows. One cannot infer this
normalization merely from positivity or normalize just the two
diagnostic categories.

Each individual birth still has both fair newborn outcomes.
The admitted maps remain scalar-passive \(q\,\mathfrak D/2\) for
admitted PSD payload \(\mathfrak D\); no informative quantum dynamics
is derived by this finite-law bound.

## 4. Actual K0/K4 masses and the common availability floor

The tested parent is \(P_4=C_4\oplus A_2\), with caps \(a,b\).
Its eight individual ideals are the five initial chain segments
(including empty and \(C_4\)), the two distinct one-cap ideals,
and the full parent. Nothing is discarded.

The empty-birth child \(K_0\) and the \(C_4\)-birth child
\(K_4=T_9\) have the intrinsic maximal-principal-downset multisets
\(\{1,5,5\}\) and \(\{5,5,5\}\), respectively.
Each comes from one individual ideal; its two newborn-bit
probabilities sum to that ideal's probability.

For \(S=\varnothing\) or \(S=C_4\), deleting either cap leaves a
five-chain, and deleting both leaves \(C_4\). Hence (8) is
\[
 u_\lambda(P_4,r,S)=
 \frac{q_\lambda(C_5,r'_a,S)\ q_\lambda(C_5,r'_b,S)}
      {q^0(C_4,r|_{C_4},S)} .
\]
The numerator slots are proper, while the denominator is full for
\(S=C_4\). Applying (3) **separately at each old marking** gives
\[
 u_\lambda(P_4,r,\varnothing)=\lambda^2\theta^2e_4,\qquad
 u_\lambda(P_4,r,C_4)=\lambda^2\theta^2c_i.                   \tag{13}
\]
No averaging of deletion factors or replacement of the full denominator
is used.

Write \(s_\lambda=\rho^H_\lambda\lambda^2\theta^2\).
The complete baseline and perturbed laws therefore have coordinates
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
  &K_0&K_4\\ \hline
 B_{\lambda,r}&s_\lambda e_4&s_\lambda c_i\\
 Q_{\lambda,r}&s_\lambda e_4&s_\lambda h_9(\lambda)c_i .
 \end{array}                                                \tag{14}
\]
All other shapes retain their full-law probabilities and are later
aggregated as OTHER, without conditioning or renormalizing (14).

The **same fixed amplitude** and (6) give
\[
 h_9(\lambda)
 \ge \frac34+\eta_*w_9
 =\frac34+\frac{\kappa_*}{\lambda_*\theta}
 >\frac74 .                                                \tag{15}
\]
The last inequality follows from \(\kappa_*>1\), \(0<\lambda_*<1\)
and \(0<\theta<10^{-10}<1\). It is an accepted symbolic multiplier
bound, not a measured or numerically evaluated probability effect.

Define
\[
                  s_*=c_4\lambda_-^2\theta^2>0.
\]
For every \(\lambda\in\mathcal I\) and every old marking,
\(s_\lambda\ge s_*\), \(c_i\ge c_1\), and (14)–(15) yield
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 B_{\lambda,r}(K_0)=Q_{\lambda,r}(K_0)\ge s_*e_4>0,\\
 B_{\lambda,r}(K_4)\ge s_*c_1>0,\qquad
 Q_{\lambda,r}(K_4)>\tfrac74s_*c_1>0,\\
 \nu_B:=B_{\lambda,r}(K_0)+B_{\lambda,r}(K_4)
       \ge s_*(e_4+c_1)=\nu_*,\\
 \nu_Q:=Q_{\lambda,r}(K_0)+Q_{\lambda,r}(K_4)
       >s_*(e_4+\tfrac74c_1)>\nu_* .
 \end{gathered}                                             \tag{16}
\]
Thus both individual diagnostic categories have positive uniform
bounds, and their sum has the common bound (1).
For any nonempty conditional class, \(\nu_*\le\nu_H<1\), since these
are actual complete probabilities and the full birth remains positive.

This is an **absolute availability** argument using actual coordinates.
Neither the normalized P4 contrast \(\delta_4/\rho\), the common
separator coefficient nor the conditional ratio gap alone supplies
(16). The added ingredients are the explicit selected-scale lower bound,
the fixed positive \(\theta\), and the positive distance of
\(\mathcal I\) from \(\lambda=0\).

## 5. Old-mark and parameter mixtures within this restriction

For a fixed \(\lambda\), any distribution over old markings changes
\(c_i\) in (14) to
\(\bar c=\pi(i=0)c_0+\pi(i=1)c_1\in[c_1,c_0]\).
The formulas were proved before mixing, so arbitrary other old-mark
correlations do not invalidate them.

More generally take a probability mixture, separately within B or Q,
over \(\lambda\in\mathcal I\) and admissible old-mark distributions,
with the **same** fixed prefix, half-scale rule, regular section and
amplitude. Require the complete shape probabilities to be measurable.
Integrating (16) preserves each non-strict floor, in particular
\(\bar\nu_H\ge\nu_*\). The perturbed strict bound also survives on
a measure concentrated on the admitted class; no zero-scale boundary
member or outside-neighborhood component is introduced.

The accepted relative inequalities remain
\[
 p_B(K_4)\le C p_B(K_0),\qquad
 p_Q(K_4)>L p_Q(K_0),\qquad
 C=\frac{648}{25},\quad L=\frac{22617}{500}.
\]
They hold componentwise and integrate as coordinate inequalities.
The conditional diagnostic label probabilities consequently satisfy
\[
 q_B:=p_B(K_4\mid K_0\text{ or }K_4)\le u=\frac{648}{673},
 \qquad
 q_Q>\ell=\frac{22617}{23117},\qquad
 \delta=\ell-u=\frac{241425}{15557741}>\frac3{200}.             \tag{17}
\]
This does not average ratios with the original mixture weights.

The two hypotheses may use different \(\lambda\)'s or marking mixtures
inside this restricted class. They need not share a nuisance value.
Mixing laws does not itself construct a new canonical native prefix or
an iid preparation process. No cross-prefix mixture is covered by the
common bound (1) unless its constituents are separately shown to share
an appropriate positive lower bound; that additional task is not done here.

## 6. Finite prospective cap with explicit error, coverage and power

Assume exact repeated observation of one fixed complete law in the
restricted class: raw categories \(X_1,X_2,\ldots\) are iid and are
classified exactly as \(K_0,K_4,\mathrm{OTHER}\).
OTHER includes every nonselected shape and every such draw counts.
This is additional access, not a conclusion from a one-birth native
law, fair newborn bits or a growing trajectory with changing parents.
No classifier, repeated preparation, detector or calibration is supplied.

Before any data, fix \(m\ge1\), a midpoint threshold
\(\tau=(u+\ell)/2\), \(k=\lceil m\tau\rceil\), conditional tolerances
\(0<\alpha_{\rm I},\alpha_{\rm II}<1\) certified below, and an abstention
target \(0<\zeta<1\).
Let \(T_m\) be the raw index of the \(m\)-th diagnostic event.
Use the accepted first-\(m\) label rule, stopping at \(T_m\wedge N\):
classify by \(S_m\ge k\) if \(T_m\le N\), otherwise ABSTAIN.
A diagnostic target reached on the final draw is classified.

For fixed iid categories, diagnostic-arrival indicators are iid
Bernoulli(\(\nu\)). Conditional on any arrival pattern with at least
\(m\) events, the first \(m\) relative labels are iid Bernoulli(\(q\)).
Thus, with \(A=\{T_m\le N\}\),
\[
 S_m\mid A\sim\operatorname{Bin}(m,q),\qquad
 P(A^c)=b_N(\nu)
       =P\{\operatorname{Bin}(N,\nu)\le m-1\}.                \tag{18}
\]
The stopping rule does not use the relative labels to choose the
diagnostic count. This is the selection justification in
[P4_DIAGNOSTIC_EVENT_TEST](P4_DIAGNOSTIC_EVENT_TEST.md), §§4–6, not an unproved optional-stopping step.

That source's self-contained concentration proof supplies
\[
 e_{\rm I},e_{\rm II}\le\exp(-m\delta^2/2).
\]
A sufficient prospective diagnostic count is
\[
 m\ge\left\lceil\frac2{\delta^2}
       \max\{\log(1/\alpha_{\rm I}),\log(1/\alpha_{\rm II})\}
       \right\rceil .                                      \tag{19}
\]
No exact tail, interval or numeric native quantity is evaluated here.

For the raw count, monotonicity and the same concentration bound give,
whenever \(N\nu_*>m-1\),
\[
 b_N(\nu)\le b_N(\nu_*)\le
 \exp\left[-2N\left(\nu_*-\frac{m-1}{N}\right)^2\right].       \tag{20}
\]
Take \(N=N_\zeta(m)\) from (2).
For \(m\ge2\), \(N\nu_*\ge2(m-1)>m-1\); for \(m=1\), \(N\ge1\)
and \(\nu_*>0\) give the strict condition.
In either case
\[
 \nu_*-\frac{m-1}{N}\ge\frac{\nu_*}{2},\qquad
 b_N(\nu)\le e^{-N\nu_*^2/2}\le\zeta.                        \tag{21}
\]
The cap is finite because the fixed \(\nu_*>0\) and \(0<\zeta<1\).
It is a conservative sufficient formula, not a minimal budget or a
runtime/physical acquisition estimate.

Let \(a_H=P_H(A)\) denote decision coverage. The distinct guarantees are:

| Quantity | Uniform guarantee on the restricted observation class |
| --- | --- |
| ABSTAIN under either label | \(P_H(A^c)\le\zeta\) |
| Decision coverage | \(a_H\ge1-\zeta>0\) |
| Conditional wrong Q under B | \(P_B(Q\mid A)\le\alpha_{\rm I}\) |
| Conditional wrong B under Q | \(P_Q(B\mid A)\le\alpha_{\rm II}\) |
| Unconditional wrong Q under B | \(P_B(Q)\le\alpha_{\rm I}a_B\le\alpha_{\rm I}\) |
| Unconditional wrong B under Q | \(P_Q(B)\le\alpha_{\rm II}a_Q\le\alpha_{\rm II}\) |
| Power under Q | \(P_Q(Q)\ge(1-\alpha_{\rm II})(1-\zeta)\) |
| Type-II failure **including ABSTAIN** | \(P_Q(B\text{ or A})\le\zeta+\alpha_{\rm II}(1-\zeta)\) |

Here output A means ABSTAIN, whereas event \(A\) in (18) means collection;
the table spells out the output where ambiguity could arise.
The last bound is \(1-(1-\alpha_{\rm II})(1-\zeta)\), not merely
\(\alpha_{\rm II}\). Coverage is lower-bounded, so it would be wrong
to replace an unconditional wrong-label upper bound by
\(\alpha_H(1-\zeta)\).

### Original design remains frozen

The original prospective example stays exactly
\[
                 m_0=40000,\quad N_0=400000,\quad k_0=38825,
 \qquad \alpha_{\rm I}=\alpha_{\rm II}=1/20.
\]
The accepted analytic label-error proof for these \(m_0,k_0\) remains
valid. This packet does **not** show that \(N_0\) has small abstention
under any native instance.

Separately, the hypothetical rule with
\(m=m_0,\ k=k_0,\ N=N_\zeta(m_0)\) has the above conditional theorem.
It is a different, unevaluated cap specified before observations,
not the original design with an after-the-fact extension.
One may declare \(\zeta=1/20\), for example, to obtain the symbolic
prospective bounds coverage \(\ge19/20\), power \(\ge361/400\) and
failure including abstention \(\le39/400\). These are design guarantees
conditional on all premises, not measured native rates or experimental
results. No such rule is implemented or authorized to run.

With general predeclared tolerances, (19) and (2) permit arbitrarily
stringent error and abstention targets within the hypothetical iid
model. They do not make the budget small, numerically available or
operationally obtainable.

### Two different mixture observation models

If the fixed law is a marginal within-label mixture described in §5
and draws are **assumed iid from that marginal**, (17)–(21) apply
with its own \(\nu,q\). Defining a marginal mixture does not prove
that the actual observations have this iid structure.

If a latent law is instead selected once per run, then observations
are only conditionally iid given that law. Under each latent component,
\(\nu\ge\nu_*\) and the same label and collection bounds hold.
Integrating gives \(P(\mathrm{ABSTAIN})\le\zeta\) and the table's
unconditional guarantees. Also
\(P(\mathrm{wrong},A\mid z)\le\alpha_H P(A\mid z)\);
integrating and dividing by positive \(P(A)\) proves the same
conditional error bound. The marginal diagnostic count is generally
not binomial at \(E\nu\); this proof does not treat it as one.
The latent distribution is within one hypothesis, not a mixture of
truth labels. Arbitrary dependent or adaptive trajectories are not covered.

For the fixed iid model the expected raw collection cost satisfies
\(E[T_m]=m/\nu\le m/\nu_*\), and the capped cost is at most
\(\min\{N,m/\nu_*\}\). The same bound follows by conditioning in the
latent model with the uniform component floor.
These are mathematical draw counts; the cost of exact order acquisition,
repeated preparation and physical readout is unknown.

## 7. Optional law choice, identities and the no-go quantifiers

The half-scale choice is **not** a coordinate relabeling.
Changing a common proper-slot scale changes actual proper-event
probabilities, the full complement and diagnostic waiting times.
Keeping a normalized ratio or a homogeneous harmonic equation unchanged
does not make those different stochastic laws the same law.

The relevant distinctions are:

| Item | Status in this theorem |
| --- | --- |
| Full complement once every proper probability is selected | Forced by normalization; not an adjustable extra slot |
| Empty-slot mark independence and transport under isomorphism | Consequences of admitted locality/equivariance |
| Held numerical values, canonical seed/selector and regular section | Particular admitted instance/construction premises, not forced by locality alone |
| Parent-five family (7) and half-scale prescription (9) | Explicit optional law choices, not uniquely selected by bare DET |
| \(v_{11}=1\) section convention | Coordinate normalization of the perturbation representation; it does not authorize changing the law or fixed amplitude |
| Positive floor (1) | Derived from the specified optional law plus the fixed-prefix and neighborhood premises |

A reciprocal rescaling of a section and its amplitude can merely
re-express the same multiplier product. That is different from changing
\(\rho\) in (14). Here neither the normalized section nor its amplitude
is rescaled.

The prior arbitrary-scale no-go says: for **every fixed finite N**,
a larger class containing a fixed eligible member's admissible sequence
\(\rho\downarrow0\) has baseline/perturbed N-observation laws approaching
each other. Uniform low false positives and high power fail on that
class; uniformly small conditional errors force coverage to vanish.

The present theorem says: **for this one fixed eligible prefix,
its fixed compact \(\mathcal I\), and the specified half-scale rule**,
there exists the finite class-dependent cap (2) with the displayed
guarantees under exact iid access. No additional factor tending to
zero may multiply \(\rho^H_\lambda\) inside this restricted class.
Its diagnostic masses cannot enter the no-go sequence, by (16).
The two statements have different domains and quantifiers and are
consistent.

Nor does (12) alone give a floor when \(\lambda\downarrow0\):
the two selected masses contain \(\lambda^2\theta^2\).
The neighborhood is deliberately bounded away from zero.
No positive floor across all eligible prefixes follows either:
the accepted sources provide no common bounds on their complete
\(m_4\), positive \(\theta\), or neighborhood endpoints.
This is absence of a cross-prefix guarantee, not construction of
a new family attaining arbitrary scalar limits.

## 8. Dependence, missing operational premises and completion

The constants depend as follows:

- \(e_4,c_0,c_1,u,\ell,\delta\) are the retained shared calibration
  and conditional-label constants.
- \(m_4\) and hence \(c_4\) depend on the **complete fixed** lower
  prefix through parent four, including all full slots and markings.
- \(\theta,\mu_i,\Xi,\lambda_*,\kappa_*\) belong to the one actual
  prefix and its own regular construction. They are not free inputs
  manufactured to satisfy displayed inequalities.
- \(\eta_*\), the available regular neighborhood, and the selected
  compact \(\mathcal I\) depend on that construction. Its
  \(\lambda_-\) remains a prefix/neighborhood-dependent positive constant.
- \(\nu_*\) combines \(c_4,\lambda_-,\theta\); \(N_\zeta(m)\) additionally
  depends on the declared diagnostic count and abstention target.
  They are uniform over the restricted marks and within-prefix
  mixtures, not over unrestricted scales or prefixes.

There is no missing algebraic relation for the **conditional restricted**
result: the accepted half-scale bound and the accepted actual empty/C4
slot identities supply precisely the absolute mass factors needed.
No coefficient reduction or additional normalization axiom is inserted.

What remains unavailable is an established actual eligible occurrence,
numerical certified values/lower bounds adequate to evaluate the cap,
and a justified repeatable exact observation/readout model.
For noncomputable or unspecified positive inputs, symbolic finiteness
is not an executable numeric-budget prescription. Compactness does not
produce a known radius or an effective numerical bound by itself.
No physical preparation, calibrated classifier or empirical channel is
provided, and no arbitrary noisy-channel transfer is inferred.

This closes the assigned law-dependence/finite-observation question:
**conditional symbolic feasibility on the selected restricted law class,
coexisting with the arbitrary-small-scale impossibility.**
It is not application advantage, operational admission, empirical
DET/ontology validation, QM reconstruction, geometry, mass/gravity
or all-size growth. No benchmark is implemented or run; the rejected
C256 raw-cost claim is not reopened.
