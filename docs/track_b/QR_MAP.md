## Definition

**Quantum instrument → event-indexed classical record → growing event order
→ question-relative summary.**

These arrows describe a composite representation, not a claim that a bare
instrument uniquely generates an order. The event-growth rule, quantum
operations and record policy must be declared separately.

A quantum instrument is a family of completely positive, trace-nonincreasing
outcome maps whose sum is trace preserving. Retain its complete
record-conditioned quantum maps, not only outcome probabilities. For each
history h, retain the unnormalized branch map J_h on arbitrary quantum inputs,
including zero branches and any declared classical growth weight. Distinct
recorded alternatives are combined by summing maps, not amplitudes.

At each stage retain the growing partial order (E_n, ≺_n), its event-indexed
setting/outcome record R_n, and the associated quantum branch maps. Births
append events and records without rewriting existing relations or records;
relabeling transports the order, record and maps together. The quantum payload
is part of this object, not discarded after producing a classical record.

For a declared question family Q, a summary σ_Q is adequate only if each
question's answer factors through σ_Q on the declared domain. Adequacy for
one Q does not imply adequacy for another. No universal geometric summary is
asserted.
