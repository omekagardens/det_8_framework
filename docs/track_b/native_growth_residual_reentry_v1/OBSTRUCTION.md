## 2. Intrinsic rectangle and corner classification

Use fixed natural labels with predecessor masks

\[
 B=(0,1,3,1,11),\quad S_0=7,\ S_1=9,\quad
 R=B+x_B=(0,1,3,1,11,31).
\]

B is the Ferrers order `(3,2)`; R is the non-chain rectangle `(3,3)`.
Both have delta zero, and R has a unique maximum, so R is noncritical.
These diagrams are combinatorial order representatives, not metric inputs.

Here is the intrinsic justification from
[RI-54](../native_growth_ferrers_bottleneck_v1/BOTTLENECK.md), rather than an
assumption that a displayed grid exhausts the possibilities. In a non-chain
Ferrers down-set, the elements with chain principal ideals form the two axis
chains, joined at their unique minimum. Removing that minimum leaves two
nonempty incomparable chains. They are intrinsic up to exchange. Every
other element is identified by its two axis initial segments. Thus an
isomorphism preserves the diagram up to transpose. Any Ferrers maximal-birth
child, after deleting its newborn, leaves a down-set isomorphic to its parent;
its new cell must be an addable corner of that intrinsic diagram.

For B the good slots are exactly the two axis ideals and the full ideal.
For R the only good slots are its two axis ideals, still masks 7 and 9.
They give `(4,3)` and `(3,3,1)` respectively. The ten ideals of R are
`0,1,3,7,9,11,15,27,31,63`. Its other eight ideal births are non-Ferrers;
the one-birth inequality `0<=delta(R+x)<=delta(R)+1` makes their defect
exactly one. Consequently, for any normalized nonnegative row at R,

\[
 \mu_{R,r+u}=1-q_{R,r+u}(S_0)-q_{R,r+u}(S_1). \tag{3}
\]

No blanket seven-event order or Ferrers catalogue is needed. The checker
checks only these ten specified ideal children and explicit isomorphisms to
the two allowed corner diagrams; exclusion of additional shapes rests on
the intrinsic lemma above, not on a two-shape enumeration pretending to be
an independent exhaustive recognizer.

## 3. Complete marked two-birth transport

For every old record `r in {0,...,31}`, put

\[
 a_{i,r}=q_{B,r}(S_i),\quad b_r=a_{0,r}+a_{1,r},\quad f_r=q_{B,r}(B).
\]

Let `C_i=B+y_(S_i)`. Compare the births `x_B` then `y_(S_i)` with `y_(S_i)`
then `x_B`, transporting the last two labels and their immutable bits. The
old B labels are fixed. Both terminal orders have x above all of B, y above
S_i, and x,y incomparable. For every old r and both newborn bits u,v, equality
of the complete maps on **every D** gives

\[
 \frac{f_r q_{R,r+u}(S_i)}4D
 =\frac{a_{i,r}q_{C_i,r+v}(B)}4D,
 \quad\text{hence}\quad
 f_r q_{R,r+u}(S_i)=a_{i,r}q_{C_i,r+v}(B). \tag{4}
\]

No off-diagonal or unnormalized payload is discarded. The factor `1/4` is
from two fair newborn bits, not a probability to be omitted from history
weights. On the left, S_i excludes u; on the right, B excludes v. Precursor
locality therefore justifies removing those bits from the corresponding
second-row weights; it is not an average over unequal record classes.

Each companion weight is at most one. Summing the two **labeled** ideals
once each and using `f_r>0` yields

\[
 g_r:=\sum_iq_{R,r+u}(S_i)\le b_r/f_r,
 \qquad \mu_{R,r+u}\ge(f_r-b_r)/f_r. \tag{5}
\]

In a strict next-layer law each companion is strictly below one, making
these inequalities strict. Boundary kernels require only non-strict bounds.
No hypothetical next-row values are presented as constructed probabilities.

## 5. Why this is not an asymptotic no-go

Let `X_n=delta_(n+1)-delta_n in {0,1}`, with natural filtration F_n and
actual conditional drift `mu_n=E[X_n|F_n]`. Since defect never decreases,

\[
 \sum_{n\ge5}\mathbf1_{\{\delta_n=0\}}X_n
 \le\mathbf1_{\{\delta_5=0\}}\le1. \tag{9}
\]

The finite rectangle obstruction belongs to this **first-exit sector**.
For its predictable hazard `H_n=1_(delta_n=0) mu_n`, tower expectation and
Tonelli give

\[
 \sum_{n\ge5}\mathbb E H_n\le\Pr(\delta_5=0)\le1,
 \qquad \sum_{n\ge5}H_n<\infty\quad\text{almost surely}. \tag{10}
\]

The realized exit count is at most one pathwise. The predictable hazard
sum need **not** be at most one on each path. Nor does a positive conditional
hazard at a specified state give recurrent visits or a persistent unconditional
lower bound. The theorem does not assert that first exit occurs almost surely.

Let `mu_n^0,mu_n^I` be the boundary and interior row drifts evaluated along
the actual history. RI-59 gives

\[
 \mu_n=(1-\eta_n)\mu_n^0+\eta_n\mu_n^I,
 \quad \eta_n=(n+1)^{-2},\quad0\le\mu_n^0,\mu_n^I\le1.
\]

For n>=5, `1-eta_n>=35/36`. Hence the **whole zero-defect boundary sector**,
not just these rectangles, satisfies

\[
 Z_n:=\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{\{\delta_n=0\}}\mu_n^0],\qquad
 \sum_{n\ge5}Z_n\le\tfrac{36}{35}\Pr(\delta_5=0)\le\tfrac{36}{35}. \tag{11}
\]

Its corresponding pathwise boundary-hazard sum is finite almost surely by
the same pointwise comparison. Again (11) is an expectation bound, not a
pathwise constant bound. This resolves the zero-defect contribution to the
asymptotic drift accounting even though it cannot be made identically zero.

## 6. One remaining proof gate: positive-defect noncritical drift

Critical parents necessarily have positive defect. By RI-61 their total
boundary contribution `C_n` is summable. Partition the canonical objective as

\[
 m_n=Z_n+C_n+T_n^+,\qquad
 T_n^+=\sum_{\substack{j:\delta(P_j)>0\\P_j\notin\mathcal C}}
            \pi_j^{(n)}\mu_j^0. \tag{12}
\]

Both Z and C are nonnegative with finite sums. Therefore the exact remaining
expectation-level condition is

\[
 \frac1N\sum_{n=5}^{N-1}T_n^+\longrightarrow0. \tag{13}
\]

By RI-59 and RI-56 this is equivalent to `delta(P_N)/N` tending to zero in
expectation, L1 and probability under the same actual candidate law.
No decay estimate for (13) is established here.

The **one justified next proof gate** is to prove or reject a proposed
history-weighted vanishing-Cesaro upper envelope for `T_n^+`, retaining
complete component coupling, all marked rows, actual history weights and
canonical selection. It must include both proper raising mixed-component
mass and full-complement raising mass. It must not demand zero drift or
restart a zero-defect rectangle certificate as an all-size argument.
This is a proof target for coordinator authorization, not a new implementation
or an asserted successful bound.

Almost-sure sublinearity has a separate pathwise condition. Set
`t_n^+=1_(delta_n>0,P_n notin C) mu_n^0`. The zero-defect and critical
boundary-hazard sums are finite almost surely, and
`sum |mu_n-mu_n^0| <= sum eta_n < infinity`. Thus
`sum |mu_n-t_n^+| < infinity` almost surely. RI-56's bounded-increment
martingale argument gives

\[
 \delta(P_N)/N\to0\ \text{a.s.}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 N^{-1}\sum_{n=5}^{N-1}t_n^+\to0\ \text{a.s.} \tag{14}
\]

A deterministic Cesaro statement about `T_n^+=E[t_n^+]` alone is not (14).
The positive finite lower bound at n=6 proves none of these limits false.
