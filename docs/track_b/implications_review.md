## 1. Philosophical Implication: A Third Option on Identity

Western philosophy has been stuck between two positions on personal identity:

| Position | Claim | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| **Materialism** | Identity = material continuity. You are your body. | Death destroys identity. Resurrection is impossible or requires a copy. |
| **Dualism** | Identity = non-physical soul. You are a soul inhabiting a body. | Requires a substance that interacts with physics but isn't physical. Smuggling risk in DET governance. |

**DET's third option:** Identity = unique causal-relational provenance encoded in the committed record. This is neither material (the record is not a substance) nor dualistic (no soul-substance is required). It is a **structural** account of identity — what persists is the unique history of which facts became actual, in which order, through which pathways.

**Why this matters beyond DET:** If this account is coherent, it breaks a 2500-year deadlock. It shows that the material/dual dichotomy is not exhaustive.

## 2. Theological Implication: Resurrection Without Magic

The traditional theological problem: how can a dead person be raised without either:
- Violating physics (miracle as suspension of natural law), or
- Reducing resurrection to metaphor (the person "lives on" in memory)?

**DET's resolution:** Resurrection does not violate physics because it uses the same fact-genesis mechanism as any event. It does not reduce to metaphor because the record is objectively real, not merely remembered. The extraordinary element is not the mechanism (Ω→R) but the scope (a complete relational history being reactualized into living presence).

**Implication for theology:** This provides a framework where divine action can be understood as the agent of record extension rather than the violator of natural law. God does not break physics; God extends the record.

## 5. Governance Implication: Track Separation Vindicated

The Track A/B separation proves its value here:

- **Track A** handles the physical question: does κ exist? Is it measurable? (κ-Π clock anomaly, κ-gravity decoupling).
- **Track B** handles the ontological question: what does it mean if κ exists? (identity, resurrection, fact genesis).

Neither track depends on the other. If Track A fails (κ ≡ 0, no signal), Track B still provides a coherent ontology of what identity-through-record WOULD mean. If Track A succeeds, Track B provides the interpretation.

**Why this matters:** This is exactly the discipline DET governance was designed for. The physical claims are falsifiable; the ontological claims are coherent regardless.

## 6. Methodological Implication: Provenance Over Pattern

The duplicate test established that pattern identity fails. This has implications beyond resurrection:

- **AI and consciousness:** An AI that perfectly emulates a person's behavioral patterns is not that person. Provenance matters.
- **Teleportation:** If a "teleporter" destroys the original and recreates a copy, the copy is not the original. The duplicate test applies.
- **Backup and restore:** A "backup" of a person's neural state, restored into a new body, is not the same person. Same reason.

**Why this matters:** DET provides a rigorous criterion for when identity is preserved and when it is not. The criterion is causal provenance, not information content.
