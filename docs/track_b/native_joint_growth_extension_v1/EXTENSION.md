24 September 2026 UTC. **Constructive finite-extension theorem; independent
coordinator adjudication accepted.** Every strictly positive prefix admitted
by [RI-34](../native_joint_growth_constraints_v1/NORMAL_FORM.md) extends through
all three-event parent rows and every two-event-base incomparable-birth
diamond. In particular, its two-chain feedback `f(0) != f(1)` survives.

This is a finite extension, not an all-size growth law, a DET derivation, a
geometry theorem or a physical prediction. The result answers the bounded
[RI-36 assignment](../../../REVIEW_IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md) in the
[native programme](../../coordination/NATIVE_GEOMETRY_GRAVITY_MEASUREMENT_PLAN.md).
Earlier accepted sources are unchanged. The proof is analytic; an isolated

for arbitrary real parameters or arbitrary full residuals.

## 1. Unchanged premises and exact target

Keep RI-34's finite committed order, independently variable binary records,
fixed full residual/type/controller/context, append-only old order and records,
one commit with total mass one, and marked-parent equivariance. Every order
ideal has strictly positive probability for every admitted record assignment.
The selector and residual update may read records only in that ideal; this is
the original [strict permission](../../validation/t8-q-licensed-growth-2026-09-12/BIRTH_F.md),
not a relative-odds replacement. No silent, halt or absorption alternative is
added. These are conditional model premises, not claims of physical availability.

The full outcome-resolved map remains

\[
 \mathcal B^{C,r}_{S,b}(D)=\tfrac12 q_{C,r}(S)D,\qquad b\in\{0,1\}.
 \tag{1}
\]

Here `q` is independent of `D`; the two newborn marks are fair. The fixed
finite carrier supports every complex Hermitian PSD kernel of total-entry
mass one, including its off-diagonal entries. Equation (1) extends to the
unnormalized cone. No composition discards its scalar weight or replaces its
payload by a diagonal or normalized summary. Positive-branch conditioning
retains exactly `D`, and the fixed context stays unchanged.

Let `r` denote the root mark of a two-chain. Retain the prefix parameters

\[
 0<a<1,\quad c>0,\quad 0<f(r)<1-c,\quad d>0,\qquad
 g=\frac{(1-a)c}{a}>0,\quad k=1-d-2g>0,
 \qquad h_r=1-c-f(r)>0.
 \tag{2}
\]

The empty parent selects its only ideal. A singleton has probabilities
`a,1-a` for its empty/full ideals. A two-chain has probabilities
`c,f(r),h_r` for empty, root and full ideals. A two-antichain has
`d,g,g,k` for empty, its two distinct singleton ideals, and full.
All these rows and their earlier diamonds are held fixed.

The new target is **rows at all parents of size three and diamonds based at
both parents of size two**, plus all earlier obligations. Largest diamond
terminals have four births. No row at a four-event parent, or diamond based
at size three, is being supplied. Weak-positivity endpoints and general
nonpassive maps are outside this assignment; every division below uses a
strictly positive quantity justified by (2).

## 2. Complete local row forms on three-event parents

There are five unmarked three-event order types. In the following table,
`e_A,e_E,e_F,e_J,e_H` are positive constants with respect to records. All
other functions may initially depend on precisely their listed precursor
marks. Arguments from different vertices are independently variable.

| Shape and relations | Proper ideals and their marginal probabilities |
|---|---|
| `A`: three-antichain | Empty: `e_A`; each singleton with mark `r`: `U(r)`; each two-vertex ideal: `V(r,s)=V(s,r)` |
| `E`: edge `u prec v`, isolated `w` | Empty: `e_E`; `{u}`: `X(r_u)`; `{w}`: `Z(r_w)`; `{u,v}`: `Y(r_u,r_v)`; `{u,w}`: `W(r_u,r_w)` |
| `F`: fork `u prec v`, `u prec w` | Empty: `e_F`; `{u}`: `P(r_u)`; either root-plus-leaf ideal: `Q(r_u,r_leaf)` |
| `J`: join `u prec w`, `v prec w` | Empty: `e_J`; either singleton root: `M(r)`; `{u,v}`: `N(r_u,r_v)=N(r_v,r_u)` |
| `H`: chain `u prec v prec w` | Empty: `e_H`; `{u}`: `T_1(r_u)`; `{u,v}`: `T_2(r_u,r_v)` |

For every row, the full-ideal probability is one minus the sum of **all
distinct labeled proper-ideal probabilities**. In particular `A` has three
singleton and three two-vertex terms, `F` has two root-plus-leaf terms, and
`J` has two singleton terms. Positivity requires every listed value and every
full-ideal complement to be positive on its complete record cube.

These are the complete row forms: ideal enumeration gives the listed slots;
strict locality excludes other marks; marked-parent equivariance identifies
only structurally exchanged slots; normalization determines the full slot.
No equality between different shapes has been assumed. A full slot can read
all records but cannot retain additional dependence after its complement is
fixed. The inherited records still pass unchanged into the child history.

## 3. All new full-payload diamond equations

Choose ordered ideals `S,T` of the **same old parent** and newborn marks
`b,t`. Append `x` at `S` and `y` at `T`, never inserting either newborn into
the other's precursor. After canonical identification of `x,y`, the target is

\[
 \mathcal B^{C+(S,b)}_{T,t}\circ\mathcal B^C_{S,b}
 =\mathcal B^{C+(T,t)}_{S,b}\circ\mathcal B^C_{T,t}.
 \tag{3}
\]

Both sides retain the identical final marked order, context and residual
carrier. Under (1), each displayed probability product below multiplies
the **entire** unnormalized `D/4`. Thus the scalar identities prove (3) on
the whole admitted domain and unnormalized cone. Mass-one `D` is nonzero,
so they are also necessary, not merely tests on a favored input. Neither
newborn mark is averaged away: the second precursor excludes that newborn,
and locality makes its forbidden record dependence absent.

### Two-chain base

Write the old parent as `u prec v` with marks `r,s`. Its ideals are
`0=empty`, `R={u}`, `B={u,v}`. The three unordered unequal pairs give

\[
 \begin{array}{lll}
 (0,R):&c\,X(r)=f(r)e_F,\\
 (0,B):&c\,Y(r,s)=h_r e_H,\\
 (R,B):&f(r)Q(r,s)=h_r T_1(r).
 \end{array}
 \tag{4}
\]

For example, adding at `0` first creates `E`; adding at `R` first creates
`F`; adding at `B` first creates `H`. The second ideal is always the
specified old-parent ideal. These observations identify every term in (4).

The equal pairs `(0,0)`, `(R,R)`, `(B,B)` give, on both routes, respectively

\[
 c e_E\,D/4,\qquad f(r)P(r)\,D/4,\qquad h_r T_2(r,s)\,D/4.
 \tag{5}
\]

They impose no extra equation. Reversing each unequal pair reverses the
same identity with newborn coordinates and records transported. This covers
all nine ordered pairs for every `r,s,b,t`.

### Two-antichain base

Let the old vertices `u,v` have marks `r,s`; its ideals are
`0=empty`, `U_0={u}`, `V_0={v}`, `B={u,v}`. The six unordered unequal pairs give

\[
 \begin{array}{lll}
 (0,U_0):&d U(r)=g e_E,&(0,V_0):\ d U(s)=g e_E,\\
 (0,B):&d V(r,s)=k e_J,\\
 (U_0,V_0):&g Z(s)=g Z(r),\\
 (U_0,B):&g W(r,s)=k M(r),&
 (V_0,B):\ g W(s,r)=k M(s).
 \end{array}
 \tag{6}
\]

Here an empty birth creates `A`, a singleton birth creates `E` with that
singleton as the edge root, and a full birth creates `J`. In particular
`W` is not assumed symmetric: its root and isolated vertex have different
roles. The two last equations preserve that distinction.

The four equal pairs give, respectively,

\[
 d e_A\,D/4,\quad g X(r)\,D/4,\quad
 g X(s)\,D/4,\quad k N(r,s)\,D/4
 \tag{7}
\]

on both routes. Reversed unequal pairs give the same transported identities.
This covers all sixteen ordered pairs, all four old-mark assignments and
all four newborn-mark pairs.

### Necessary and sufficient finite classification

Equations (4) and (6), the row forms in section 2, and strict positivity
of every row value are necessary and sufficient for this extension problem.
Necessity follows by listing every ideal pair as above. For sufficiency,
(4) and (6) cover all unequal pairs; (5) and (7) cover all equal ones;
reversal covers the remaining orientations. Row normalization is built into
the full complements, and locality/equivariance are built into the row forms.
Earlier rows and identities remain unchanged. This is a classification of

By strict positivity, (4) forces `Y` to ignore its second argument and `Q`

The following construction demonstrates feasibility, rather than inferring

## 4. A positive extension for every admitted prefix

Define the fixed parameter

\[
 M_* =\max\left\{1,\frac gd,\frac kd,\frac kg,
       \frac{f(0)}c,\frac{f(1)}c,
       \frac{h_0}c,\frac{h_1}c,
       \frac{h_0}{f(0)},\frac{h_1}{f(1)}\right\},
 \qquad \varepsilon=\frac1{8M_*}.
 \tag{8}
\]

All entries are finite and positive by (2), and `M_*>=1`. Choose this number
**once from the fixed prefix parameter table**, before evaluating any current
record assignment. The constants `f(0),f(1)` describe that table, not two
records read from the current parent. No row re-estimates `epsilon` from its
actual marks or introduces a record-dependent global normalizer.

Set the proper-ideal probabilities as follows, keeping the full complement
convention from section 2:

| Shape | Proper-ideal probabilities of the extension |
|---|---|
| `A` | Empty: `epsilon`; each singleton: `epsilon*g/d`; each pair: `epsilon*k/d` |
| `E` | Empty and `{w}`: `epsilon`; `{u}`: `epsilon*f(r_u)/c`; `{u,v}`: `epsilon*h_(r_u)/c`; `{u,w}`: `epsilon*k/g` |
| `F` | Empty and `{u}`: `epsilon`; each root-plus-leaf ideal: `epsilon*h_(r_u)/f(r_u)` |
| `J` | Empty, both singleton roots, and the root pair: `epsilon` each |
| `H` | Empty, root and two-vertex prefix: `epsilon` each |

The corresponding full-ideal probabilities are exactly

\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 A:&1-\varepsilon[1+3g/d+3k/d],\\
 E:&1-\varepsilon[2+(1-c)/c+k/g],\\
 F:&1-\varepsilon[2+2h_r/f(r)],\\
 J:&1-4\varepsilon,\\
 H:&1-3\varepsilon.
 \end{array}
 \tag{9}
\]

For `E`, the cancellation uses `f(r)+h_r=1-c`; for `F`, the two equal
root-plus-leaf terms are both counted. Every proper-ideal value is positive
and at most `epsilon*M_* = 1/8`. There are at most seven proper ideals,
so every full complement is at least `1/8`, hence strictly positive. This
proves normalization and positivity on **every** admitted record assignment,
not only the all-zero row or chosen numerical fixtures.

Locality holds: varying expressions in `E` and `F` read only their root
mark, and that root belongs to the corresponding precursor. Full ideals may
read their entire parent. All other entries are mark-independent. Structural
roles, not birth labels, select the formula, and exchanged leaves/roots have
identical formulas; hence complete marked-parent equivariance holds.

Substituting these rows into the three chain equations gives

\[
 c\frac{\varepsilon f(r)}c=f(r)\varepsilon,\qquad
 c\frac{\varepsilon h_r}c=h_r\varepsilon,\qquad
 f(r)\frac{\varepsilon h_r}{f(r)}=h_r\varepsilon.
 \tag{10}
\]

The antichain equations reduce to

\[
 d\frac{\varepsilon g}d=g\varepsilon,\qquad
 d\frac{\varepsilon k}d=k\varepsilon,\qquad
 g\varepsilon=g\varepsilon,\qquad
 g\frac{\varepsilon k}g=k\varepsilon,
 \tag{11}
\]

including the exchanged singleton instances. Equations (5) and (7) already
handle equal precursors. Each identity multiplies the entire `D/4` under
(1), so it holds for both newborn marks and every complex PSD input kernel.
Together with the inherited obligations this proves the stated extension
theorem for arbitrary real prefix parameters satisfying (2).

All-zero marks are included explicitly in the universal substitutions.
No ideal or history is identified with another before accounting for its
labeled weight; the two singleton transitions of the old antichain and the
three singleton transitions of `A` remain separate. Normalized residual
agreement alone is never used as a covariance test.
