## 2. Theorem: what the infinite catalogue does and does not identify

For \(\mu\in\mathcal M\) and (3), define the probability measure
\[
 \nu=\frac f t\,\delta_1+\frac{t-f}{t}\mu.                           \tag{5}
\]
Then the catalogue (4) uniquely identifies t and nu, but in general not
f and mu separately. Indeed
\[
                       q_1=t,\qquad
                q_{k+1}/t=\int\theta^k d\nu,\quad k\ge0.           \tag{6}
\]
Moment uniqueness on [0,1] is proved in section4.

More precisely, if \(v=\nu(\{1\})\), the full equivalence class of responses

\[
 0\le f\le tv,\qquad
 \mu_f=\frac{t\nu-f\delta_1}{t-f},\qquad t=q_1.                     \tag{7}
\]
Here \(\nu((0,1))>0\), so \(v<1\), ensuring \(f<t\) throughout the interval.
For each listed f the numerator is a nonnegative measure of total mass
t-f, and \(\mu_f((0,1))=t\nu((0,1))/(t-f)>0\). Thus every member is a
valid member of the declared family with positive response slope.
Conversely (5) forces (7), and nonnegativity of its atom at1 forces
\(f\le tv\). No other ambiguities occur within this observation model.

If absence of an endpoint atom is independently imposed on every candidate,
\[
                            \mu(\{1\})=0,                         \tag{8}
\]
then the full triple (f,t,mu) is uniquely identifiable from the infinite

\[

The denominator is strictly positive. No finite-budget estimator, rate,

## 4. Compact-interval moment uniqueness

Suppose two Borel probability measures lambda and rho on [0,1] have the
same moments of every nonnegative integer order. They integrate every
polynomial equally. For a continuous real function g, put
\(M=\sup|g|\) and define its Bernstein polynomial
\[
 B_mg(x)=\sum_{j=0}^m g(j/m)\binom mj x^j(1-x)^{m-j}.
\]
The finite binomial weights have mean x and variance x(1-x)/m<=1/(4m)
for the variable j/m. For any delta>0, the total weight at
\(|j/m-x|>\delta\) is at most \(1/(4m\delta^2)\): multiply each such
weight by its squared displacement and sum. Thus
\[
 \sup_x|B_mg(x)-g(x)|
 \le\omega_g(\delta)+\frac{M}{2m\delta^2},                          \tag{14}
\]
where \(\omega_g(\delta)=\sup_{|u-v|\le\delta}|g(u)-g(v)|\).
Uniform continuity makes the first term arbitrarily small; then taking
m large makes the second small. Hence \(B_mg\to g\) uniformly.
Since both measures have mass one, equality of polynomial integrals
implies equality of integrals of every continuous g.

For each a<1, use continuous functions equal to1 on [0,a], decreasing
linearly to0 on [a,a+epsilon], and zero above, with epsilon decreasing
to zero inside (0,1-a). Their bounded pointwise limit is the indicator
of [0,a], including any atom at a. The measures therefore agree on
[0,a]. At a=1 they agree by normalization. Differences give agreement
on every half-open interval (a,b], with [0,b] covering the endpoint0.
These intervals generate the Borel sets, so finite-measure uniqueness
gives equality of lambda and rho.

The measure-theoretic foundations used here are bounded convergence for
finite measures and uniqueness of finite measures agreeing on the
interval generating class (equivalently the elementary pi-lambda
uniqueness theorem). The specialized moment-uniqueness step is proved
above, not cited as an unverified reconstruction theorem. The same
bounded-convergence fact justifies (13). No numerical polynomial or
scientific executor was used.

# CE-T001 — fixed-DAG payload-update correctness and charged work

**Status:** one manual, conditional proof obligation closed for admitted fixed finite DAGs and `P` edits only. No source, fixture, hash, timing, or scientific computation was run. CE-R001's execution HOLD and the three-arm M1 HOLD remain in force. This is a conventional incremental-cache theorem, not a DET-specific mechanism or a speed/energy result.

## Exact domain and recurrence

Adopt the final CE-D001 specification: a validated DAG `G=(V,E)`, `n∈{128,512,2048}`, at most 65,536 distinct edges, payload lengths 0–1024 bytes, and fixed sink targets `T={n−16,...,n−1}`. Edges and targets remain fixed for the entire theorem. Let `A=Anc_G(T)` include the targets. Since `A` is predecessor-closed, every predecessor of a node in `A` is also in `A`. The initial fixture must be admitted; the subsequent finite sequence consists only of `P` frames. A well-framed `P` with a valid ID and payload length ≤1024 is accepted, including a byte-identical replacement. A well-framed semantic violation receives `R` and leaves all published state unchanged. Malformed/truncated frames are parser or fixture errors, not semantic `R` edits, and emit no edit record; initial admission and the separate CE-D003 parser-negative path retain their stated behavior.

For each node `x`, in ascending predecessor-ID order, use **exactly**

`K_x = SHA256(ASCII("CE-K1") || U32(x) || U16(|P_x|) || P_x || U16(|pred(x)|) || concat_{u∈pred(x)}(U32(u)||K_u))`.

`SHA256` is the specified finite byte function with a 32-byte result. Key equality is equality of these defined bytes, even if two distinct framed inputs collide. Initial output is `0x49` plus the 16 target keys in ascending target order; every processed edit emits `A` or `R` plus those same 16 positions. Each record is exactly 513 bytes.

## Concrete schedule and invariant

First validate the entire initial graph and stream prefix, build reverse adjacency for `A`, forward adjacency for scheduling, a valid topological order and rank, and compute each `K_x` for `x∈A` from raw payloads in that order. Charge all this cold work. Retain the exact payload state, `A` membership, and a 32-byte cached key for every `x∈A`. **Invariant before each edit:** published graph/payloads satisfy CE-D001 admission, the graph and `A` match the fixed initial graph, and every required cached key equals the recurrence on the published state.

For each fully decoded `P(v,p')`, validate before mutation. If invalid, emit `R` with the prior 16 target keys. If valid, stage the new payload and any changed keys privately. If `p'` is byte-identical to the old payload, commit the accepted state and emit `A` with old target keys; the payload comparison itself is charged. If `v∉A`, commit the new payload and emit `A` with unchanged target keys. Otherwise place `v` in a min-heap ordered by the fixed topological rank and mark it queued. Repeatedly pop the lowest-rank node `x`, clear its queued mark, frame its new recurrence input using the staged payload and each predecessor's staged key if present (otherwise the old cache), hash it, and compare its 32 bytes with the old cached key. Stage the result. **Only when those key bytes differ**, inspect each outgoing edge `(x,y)` with `y∈A` and enqueue `y` unless already queued. The queued mark deduplicates multiple changed predecessors. On successful completion, atomically publish the payload and staged cache and emit `A` with target keys; if staging, hashing, or publication fails, abort the stream rather than reporting a semantic `R` or partial success. The output must not precede publication.

Every predecessor of `x` has lower topological rank. A changed predecessor that can enqueue `x` is processed before `x` is popped; any chain that could reveal another changed predecessor also has increasing ranks below `x`. Thus `x` is popped after all potentially changed predecessors and, with deduplication, at most once per accepted edit. No pending higher-rank node can change an already-popped predecessor. The schedule obtains affected work from charged adjacency scans; it does not receive an affected-set oracle.

## Inductive equality with competent from-scratch recomputation

Base: topological evaluation of the validated initial `A` uses the exact recurrence and predecessor-closed set, so the invariant holds and initial 513-byte output equals a fresh evaluation of all required keys.

Step: assume the invariant before one edit. A semantic rejection publishes nothing, so the invariant and the `R` record follow. A malformed frame also publishes nothing and follows the separate parser-error contract. For an accepted identical payload, the recurrence inputs are unchanged; `A` output and the invariant remain correct despite status `A`. For accepted off-cone `v`, no node of `A` depends on `v`, by predecessor closure, so committing that payload changes no required key.

For an accepted changed payload inside `A`, consider nodes in fixed topological order. At `v` the schedule recomputes the exact new recurrence input. At any other `x`, its own payload and predecessor list are unchanged. If no predecessor key changed, every byte of its recurrence input is unchanged, so the old cache already equals the new defined key and no processing is necessary. If a predecessor key changed, that predecessor scans `(u,x)` and enqueues `x`; the heap order and deduplication ensure that `x` is recomputed exactly once after all such predecessors. Its staged result therefore equals from-scratch `K_x` on the new state. If that result equals the old cached bytes, including a genuine SHA-256 collision, no successor needs notification: its `U32(x)||K_x` input bytes are unchanged. Induction over `A` restores the invariant. The fixed target keys then give the same `A` record as a competent fresh recomputation on the accepted state. This establishes equality of all initial and per-edit **defined output bytes** for every finite admitted payload-only sequence, without assuming collision resistance or artifact equivalence.

This proof does not cover `I`/`D` graph edits, changing target ancestry, dynamic topological order, cache eviction/re-entry after topology changes, generator correctness, parser implementation, concurrent mutations, crash durability, or any actual implementation. The final CE-D001 spec stated the needed induction obligation but explicitly did not claim a completed proof. CE-T001 closes only its fixed-graph payload-only slice. The broader graph-edit proof and independent byte-for-byte correctness checks remain open.

## Charged operation ledger

Let `a=|A|`, `e_A=|E∩(A×A)|`, `d^-(x)=|pred(x)|`. An actual hash of node `x` consumes exactly `L_j(x)=13+|P_x^{(j)}|+36d^-(x)` framed input bytes and returns 32 key bytes: `"CE-K1"` 5 + ID 4 + payload length 2 + payload + predecessor count 2 + `(ID 4 + key 32)` per predecessor. For a set `S`, `B_j(S)=Σ_{x∈S}L_j(x)`. Let `W_j` be nodes actually popped/hashed by the incremental schedule, `w_j=|W_j|≤a`, and `s_j` the number of outgoing edges actually inspected from nodes whose key bytes changed. Heap insertion attempts are ≤`s_j+1`; each node enters at most once. A no-op, rejection, or off-cone payload edit has `W_j=∅` with the stated shortcuts, but still incurs parsing/validation/output and possibly cone/index checks.

Every edit, including `R` and accepted no-op, emits another 513 bytes; over a `q`-edit admitted stream both arms produce exactly `513(q+1)` bytes. Rejected edits do not hash keys under the stated shortcut, but still decode, validate, serialize, and preserve state. The later CE-D003 **correctness-only** CEC1 vectors have `0≤q≤8`, a separate ≤1-MiB fixture allocation and five actors including an oracle; they are not padded to 64 edits and have no timing result. The prospective CED1 performance stream has exactly 64 edits and its original 30-minute/one-CPU/1-GiB budget is still ungranted. No correctness-vector allowance is a benchmark run grant.

For a finite horizon `H` (64 for a prospective CED1 stream), let `C_F^0,C_I^0` be all cold costs, including graph/cone/index setup, initial hashes, initial 513-byte output and retained allocation. Let `N_{F,j},N_{I,j}` be each arm's **non-hash** per-edit costs (validation, cone traversal or charged maintenance, topological/order work, staging, discovery/worklist/key comparison, output/custody, cleanup), and let `H_j(S)` denote actual cost of the `|S|` SHA-256 calls over exactly `B_j(S)` framed bytes. The end-to-end incremental arm is cheaper **only if**

`Σ_{j=1..H}[H_j(A_j) − H_j(W_j)] > (C_I^0 − C_F^0) + Σ_{j=1..H}(N_{I,j} − N_{F,j})`,

where `A_j=A` for real accepted `P` changes, and the hash saving is defined as zero for common rejected/no-op shortcuts; use the actual F policy for any off-cone shortcut. This is an accounting identity/conditional break-even test, not a bound on wall time. For F0 versus I0, both full cone walks appear on their charged sides and may largely cancel, while F0's order rebuild and I0's worklist/cache operations do not. If F makes a charged full walk while I uses a maintained cone, I must pay initial cone/index setup and memory; if a separately stipulated maintained-cone F variant reuses its cone/order, neither gets a free repeated-walk saving; the frozen F1 reuses order only and continues to pay ancestry traversal. I1 additionally charges its forward reachability count and, if its fixed predicate fires, the full-cone fallback *after* discovery; this theorem's key equality still applies, but no uncharged fallback saving is assumed.

**Manual structural counterweight.** In CE-D001's admitted sparse `n=128` base, `L=7`; the 16 chains plus target edges and 15 cross-links give 128 target ancestors and 127 edges. A changed `P(97,p')` has only node 97 and target 113 on its downstream path; barring equal-key stopping, `W` has two nodes while F hashes the 128-node cone. Yet on a one-edit horizon I must still build/retain indexes and 128 cached keys, I0 must reverse-walk that cone, and it must schedule/compare/serialize. The saved 126 hashes may or may not cover those actual costs. With an identical `P(97,p)` both arms take the charged no-op shortcut and save no warm hashes at all, while I still paid its cold cache cost. Thus structural locality does not prove end-to-end speed, an asymptotic universal advantage, or lower joules. No latency, memory peak, energy, crossover, or 0.85 engineering threshold was measured.
